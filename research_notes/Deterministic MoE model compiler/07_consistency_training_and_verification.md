# Consistency Training and Output-Parity Verification for LLMs (state as of Oct 2026)

Scope: (a) training-side methods that make models intrinsically more consistent or deterministic; (b) measurement, verification and certification methods for "output parity", meaning compiler-like trust. There are three levels of parity: same input gives the same output (bitwise); semantically equivalent inputs give equivalent outputs (semantic); and outputs provably satisfy a spec (verified). Each finding is dated by publication or version date. Numbers are the authors' own unless marked otherwise. Research for these notes was done on 2026-10-21.

---

## 1. Training for consistency and invariance: which training methods make models intrinsically more consistent?

### Takeaway
There are three families of consistency training, and as of Oct 2026 each has quantitative evidence:
- **Invariance training.** Self-supervised consistency targets: the model's own clean-prompt answer is the target for a perturbed prompt (BCT, ACT, F²C).
- **Entropy and mode-seeking objectives.** Entropy minimization, majority-vote RL, reverse-KL on-policy distillation and RLVR all collapse the output distribution.
- **Determinism-aware training infrastructure.** Batch-, TP- and precision-invariant kernels make the sampler and the trainer numerically identical, so on-policy RL really is on-policy.

The catch is that entropy and diversity collapse is the same mechanism that buys consistency. Several papers show it caps exploration and pass@k, reduces creative diversity, and hurts calibration. Training for consistency therefore has to be paired with calibration and abstention or clarification training.

### Cited Findings

**A. Invariance / consistency regularization (paraphrase, bias, sycophancy, jailbreak wrappers)**
- (Mar 2024, rev. 2025) **Bias-augmented Consistency Training (BCT).** This unsupervised fine-tuning method trains a model to give the same reasoning with or without a biasing feature in the prompt. The dataset covers 9 bias types on GPT-3.5-Turbo and Llama-8B. On GPT-3.5-Turbo trained against one bias, BCT cut biased reasoning by 86% on held-out tasks and by 37% on average on held-out biases. No gold labels are needed. — [Chua et al., arXiv 2403.05518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05518v2)
- (arXiv Oct 2025; AF post 4 Nov 2025) **GDM "Consistency Training Helps Stop Sycophancy and Jailbreaks".** The paper frames consistency training as a *self-supervised* paradigm: the target is the model's own response to the clean prompt (the prompt without the cue). It has two variants:
  - **BCT** works on output tokens.
  - **Activation Consistency Training (ACT)** applies an L2 loss that pushes residual-stream activations on wrapped prompts toward those on clean prompts.

  Models tested: Gemma 2 (2B, 27B), Gemma 3 (4B, 27B) and Gemini 2.5 Flash. — [Irpan, Turner et al., arXiv 2510.27062](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.27062); [Alignment Forum summary](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/DLrQ2jjijqpX78mHJ/gdm-consistency-training-helps-limit-sycophancy-and)
  - On Gemini 2.5 Flash, BCT cut ClearHarm attack success rate from **67.8% to 2.9%**. ACT reduced jailbreaks less, but rarely made the model refuse more benign prompts (measured on XSTest and WildJailbreak). BCT and ACT reduced sycophancy (MMLU with user-suggested wrong answers) about equally. — [AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/DLrQ2jjijqpX78mHJ/gdm-consistency-training-helps-limit-sycophancy-and)
  - SFT on stale targets generated by GPT-3.5-Turbo was strictly worse than BCT. The authors name two failure modes of static datasets: "specification staleness" (outdated guidelines) and "capability staleness" (targets from weaker models). Self-generated targets avoid both. — [AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/DLrQ2jjijqpX78mHJ/gdm-consistency-training-helps-limit-sycophancy-and)
- (16 Oct 2025, v2 Oct 2026, ACL 2026) **Flip-Flop Consistency (F²C).** This is unsupervised training for robustness to prompt perturbations. It combines two losses:
  - **Consensus Cross-Entropy:** a majority vote across prompt variants gives a hard pseudo-label.
  - **Representation-alignment loss:** pulls low-confidence and non-majority variants toward the consensus.

  The evaluation used 11 datasets across 4 tasks, with 4–15 prompt variants each. Agreement across variants rose by **11.62%** on average, mean F1 rose by **8.94%**, and variance across prompt formats fell by **3.29%**. The gains held on held-out formats and out of domain. — [Hejabi et al., arXiv 2510.14242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14242)
- (Nov 2025) **RoParQ.** This is paraphrase-aware SFT with an XParaCon cross-paraphrase consistency metric. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, XParaCon rose from 2.186 to 2.629 (abstract-level figure). — [arXiv 2511.21568](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21568v1)
- (Mar 2026) **Mechanistically guided LoRA with a consistency loss** on MedGemma-4B for paraphrased medical yes/no questions. The baseline paraphrase flip rate was 14.6% (from a search summary; full text not verified). — [arXiv 2603.00148](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00148)
- (Nov 2023) **FlipFlop experiment (sycophancy under "Are you sure?").** Ten LLMs on 7 classification tasks flipped their answers **46%** of the time on average, with an average accuracy drop of **17%**. Models flipped wrong answers more often than right ones. Fine-tuning on synthetic data cut the deterioration by **60%** but did not eliminate it. — [Laban et al., arXiv 2311.08596](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.08596)

**B. Self-consistency used as a training signal (distilling majority vote into the policy)**
- (Nov 2024; ICML 2025) **Self-Consistency Preference Optimization (ScPO).** ScPO builds preference pairs from the most self-consistent response (chosen) and the least self-consistent one (rejected), with a confidence-weighted loss. It moves self-consistency from inference time to training time, so it adds no inference compute. Llama-3 8B gained **+6.5%** on ZebraLogic, beating Llama-3 70B, Gemma-2 27B and Claude-3 Haiku. — [Prasad et al., ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/prasad25a.html)
- (Apr 2025; NeurIPS 2025) **TTRL.** Majority vote over rollouts serves as the pseudo-label for rule-based RL rewards on unlabeled test data. Pass@1 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B on AIME 2024 rose by about **211%**, and the trained model can exceed its own maj@n signal. — [Zuo et al., arXiv 2504.16084](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16084)

**C. Entropy minimization / low-entropy fine-tuning**
- (May 2025; NeurIPS 2025) **"The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Entropy Minimization in LLM Reasoning".** Three variants:
  - **EM-FT:** token-level entropy minimization on the model's own unlabeled outputs.
  - **EM-RL:** negative entropy as the only reward.
  - **EM-INF:** inference-time logit adjustment.

  Results:
  - EM-RL on Qwen-7B matches or beats GRPO and RLOO trained on 60K labeled examples.
  - EM-INF lets Qwen-32B match or exceed GPT-4o, Claude 3 Opus and Gemini 1.5 Pro on SciCode, at about 3x the efficiency of self-consistency.

  The working assumption is that confident outputs are more likely correct. — [Agarwal et al., arXiv 2505.15134](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15134v1); [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117754)
- (May 2025, v4 Aug 2025) **One-shot Entropy Minimization.** The authors trained **13,440** models to average out training randomness. **One unlabeled example and 10 optimization steps** gave gains "comparable to or even greater than" rule-based RL with thousands of examples. — [Gao et al., arXiv 2505.20282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20282)

**D. RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR): entropy collapse and diversity**
- (May 2025) **The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning LMs.**
  - Policy entropy collapses sharply early in RL, and performance plateaus with it.
  - An empirical law fits performance to entropy as **R = −a·e^H + b**. Performance is therefore capped when H reaches 0.
  - Entropy change is driven by the covariance between an action's log-probability and its advantage, which stays mostly positive during training.
  - Fixes: **Clip-Cov** and **KL-Cov** restrict updates on high-covariance tokens. Both are merged into verl.

  — [Cui et al., arXiv 2505.22617 / HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.22617); [verl docs](https://verl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo/entropy.html)
- (Apr 2025; NeurIPS 2025 oral) **"Does RL Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity…?"**
  - RLVR models beat their base models at small k (pass@1), but base models score higher at large k.
  - The reasoning paths that RLVR models produce are already in the base model's sampling distribution.
  - The reasoning boundary often *narrows* as RLVR training goes on.

  — [Yue et al., arXiv 2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13837)
- (May 2025; NeurIPS 2025) **ProRL partly contradicts this.** Prolonged RL with KL control, reference-policy resets and a diverse task suite beat base models across pass@k, including tasks where the base model fails at any k. Gains are negatively correlated with how strong the base model already is, and narrowing still appears on math. The model is 1.5B. — [Liu et al., arXiv 2505.24864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24864)
- (Oct 2023; ICLR 2024) **RLHF and diversity.** RLHF significantly reduces output diversity compared with SFT across a range of measures. It generalizes better out of distribution, which the authors describe as a generalization–diversity trade-off. — [Kirk et al., arXiv 2310.06452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06452v3)
- (Oct 2025; ICML 2026) **Verbalized Sampling.** The authors attribute post-training mode collapse to *typicality bias in preference data*: annotators favor familiar text. They verified this on UltraFeedback, HelpSteer-v2 and Skywork, and larger models showed stronger bias. In other words, collapse is a data-level effect, not only an algorithmic one. — [Zhang et al., arXiv 2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171v1)

**E. Mode-seeking distillation**
- (27 Oct 2025) **On-Policy Distillation (Thinking Machines).**
  - Method: the student samples its own trajectories, and the loss is per-token **reverse KL** to a fixed teacher, with discount 0.
  - Reverse KL is mode-seeking: the student "learns one specific behavior" instead of spreading probability mass across options.
  - AIME'24 (TML's own runs): SFT on 400K prompts reached 60%, RL 68%, and on-policy distillation **70% in ~150 steps**. That is a **9–30x** FLOP reduction against an extrapolated SFT-2M baseline.
  - Figures cited from the Qwen3 report: RL 67.6% at 17,920 GPU-hours versus RL followed by on-policy distillation 74.4% at 1,800 GPU-hours.
  - Recovering after mid-training: IFEval 79% to 83% while internal-QA knowledge went from 36% to 41%.

  — [Kevin Lu / Thinking Machines blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
  - In the same post, SFT on the model's *own* samples degraded IFEval at every learning rate above zero. The authors attribute this to finite-batch noise turning self-training off-policy. This is a caution for naive "self-distill for consistency" schemes. — [same](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)

**F. Calibration, abstention and clarification training**
- (Jul 2025; ICLR 2026) **RLCR.** The reward is binary correctness plus a **Brier score** on a verbalized confidence. The authors prove that any bounded proper scoring rule yields a policy that is both accurate and calibrated. Ordinary RL *degrades* calibration, while RLCR improves it at the same accuracy. MIT's press release reports up to **90%** lower calibration error on a 7B model (press figure, Apr 2026). — [Damani et al., arXiv 2507.16806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16806); [MIT News 2026-04-22](https://news.mit.edu/2026/teaching-ai-models-to-say-im-not-sure-0422)
- (May 2025) Pre-trained LLMs are usually well calibrated, but preference-aligned models become poorly calibrated, as first observed for GPT-4 with RLHF. The paper describes this as universal across alignment methods. — [Restoring Calibration for Aligned LLMs, arXiv 2505.01997](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01997v3)
- (May 2023) Verbalized confidence from RLHF models can be better calibrated than the token probabilities. The paper reports ECE and ECE after temperature scaling (ECE-t). — [Tian et al., arXiv 2305.14975](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14975)
- (5 Sep 2025) **OpenAI, "Why language models hallucinate".** Accuracy-only scoring rewards guessing. On SimpleQA, GPT-5-thinking-mini abstained **52%** of the time with a **26%** error rate, against **1%** abstention and **75%** error for o4-mini, while o4-mini's accuracy was slightly higher. The proposal is to score errors worse than abstentions. — [OpenAI blog](https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/)
- (Jul 2025) **Clarification via RL.** PPO with a reward for questions that go beyond the given context. Qwen-2.5-7B beat o3-mini by 18% on automatic metrics, and humans preferred its clarifying questions 42% of the time. SFT alone did not reliably produce the behavior. — [arXiv 2507.21389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21389)
- (Dec 2025) **SpeakRL** uses RLVR rewards for *when* and *what* to ask. — [arXiv 2512.13159](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13159v1)
- (Feb 2026) **AskBench** gives a rubric-guided RLVR reward that penalizes premature answers on underspecified or false-premise queries, using a simulated user. — [arXiv 2602.11199](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11199v1)

**G. Determinism-aware training (training–inference numeric alignment)**
- (10 Sep 2025) **Thinking Machines, "Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference".**
  - Root cause: the kernels are not **batch-invariant**. Server load changes the batch size, which changes reduction order in RMSNorm, matmul (split-K / stream-K) and attention (split-KV must use a fixed split *size*).
  - "True on-policy RL" experiment (Qwen2.5-VL 8B, Bigmath RLVR):
    - Without importance weighting, reward collapses and sampler/trainer KL spikes (loss spike around step 318).
    - With importance weighting, KL stays around **0.001**.
    - With batch-invariant kernels, KL is **flat at 0**.
  - Cost on Qwen3-8B: 26 s (default vLLM), 55 s (unoptimized deterministic), 42 s (improved attention kernel).

  — [Horace He / TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference)
- (22 Sep 2025) **SGLang + slime.**
  - Deterministic mode extends TML's batch-invariant operators and supports CUDA graphs, chunked prefill and seeded sampling at temperature > 0 (via a seeded Gumbel-noise sampler).
  - Overhead averages **34.35%** with FlashInfer/FA3, against TML's reported **61.5%**.
  - Two independent Qwen3-8B GRPO training runs gave **identical curves**. Megatron deterministic mode and PyTorch deterministic algorithms were also needed.

  — [LMSYS blog](https://lmsys.org/blog/2025-09-22-sglang-deterministic/); [SGLang docs](https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/deterministic_inference.html)
- (2025–2026) vLLM ships the mode as an opt-in, enabled with `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1`. Hardware requirements conflict across doc versions: compute capability ≥8.0 on the stable page, ≥9.0 (H100/H200/B100/B200) on the latest and v0.12–v0.22 pages. DeepSeek-V3/R1/V3.1 are listed as verified. — [vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/features/batch_invariance/); [vLLM latest](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/batch_invariance/)
- verl documents a "Full Determinism for Reproducible RL Training" mode. — [verl docs](https://verl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/advance/determinism.html)
- (30 Oct 2025) **Defeating the Training-Inference Mismatch via FP16.** BF16 rounding is the root cause of the rollout/trainer mismatch, and switching to FP16 removes it with a few lines of code. Plain importance-weighted policy gradient in FP16 beat all BF16 baselines, including those with token-level and sequence-level IS corrections (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B). — [Qi et al., arXiv 2510.26788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26788)
- (Jun 2025; NeurIPS 2025 oral) **"Give Me FP32 or Give Me Death?".** Changing batch size, GPU count or GPU type shifts outputs. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B under BF16 greedy decoding showed up to **9%** accuracy variation. **LayerCast** keeps BF16 weights and does all computation in FP32; it ships as a vLLM patch. — [Yuan et al., arXiv 2506.09501](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09501)
- (ICML 2026, Jul 2026) **Tree-Based Invariant Kernels (TBIK).** Triton matmul and reduction kernels with a fixed reduction order within and across GPUs. The result is bitwise-identical outputs across **tensor-parallel sizes** and "zero probability divergence" between FSDP training (TP=1) and multi-GPU TP rollout, integrated into vLLM and FSDP. — [Zhang et al., ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66224)
- (22 Sep 2026) **ReproFuse.** Fixed-configuration, fused-upcast GEMMs: 16-bit weights are upcast to FP32 in registers and accumulated with IEEE-754 arithmetic, so reduction order depends only on shape. Linear-layer outputs are **bitwise identical across NVIDIA Ampere, Ada and Hopper**. It is **1.17–3.1x** faster than the prior state of the art and halves weight-memory traffic. — [Cooper et al., arXiv 2609.25624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25624)

### Inferences
- A compiler-grade training recipe is a stack, not a single technique:
  1. Deterministic, batch/TP/precision-invariant numerics in both trainer and sampler (TBIK, ReproFuse, FP16, SGLang/vLLM batch-invariant modes). This makes RL truly on-policy and training runs bit-reproducible.
  2. Self-supervised invariance losses over a library of semantics-preserving transformations (BCT/ACT/F²C). These turn "equivalent inputs give equivalent outputs" into a trained property, with targets regenerated from the current model to avoid staleness.
  3. Mode-seeking objectives (reverse-KL on-policy distillation, ScPO, TTRL, entropy minimization) to concentrate probability mass on one canonical answer.
  4. Proper-scoring-rule calibration rewards (RLCR) plus abstention and clarification rewards, so that low-entropy outputs stay *calibrated*. A low-entropy model that is confidently wrong is the worst case for a "compiler".
- Entropy minimization and RLVR improve repeatability at pass@1 because they collapse onto the mode, but Cui et al.'s R = −a·e^H + b law and Yue et al.'s pass@k result imply a ceiling. A practical design may train a high-entropy "explorer" and distill it on-policy into a low-entropy "compiler" checkpoint, keeping the two roles separate.
- BCT-style self-targets avoid capability staleness, which suits a model ecosystem that is upgraded often: the invariance spec (the set of transformations) is versioned, while the targets are regenerated per checkpoint.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports TARr@N / TARa@N *before vs after* consistency training. Invariance training papers report agreement or flip rate under perturbation, not bitwise repeatability.
- No published per-number BCT over-refusal figure for Gemini 2.5 Flash was found in the summaries reviewed. The arXiv HTML fetch was blocked, so the full tables in arXiv 2510.27062 were not checked.
- TBIK reports no overhead figures at the abstract level. One-shot EM's benchmark numbers were not in the abstract.
- No public evidence found on whether frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) use batch-invariant training kernels in production RL. Only open-source stacks (SGLang/slime, vLLM/TorchTitan, verl) are documented.
- Calibration (ECE) results after entropy-minimization training were not found. This interaction is untested in the sources reviewed.

## 2. Measuring consistency and parity: what metrics and statistical tests exist?

### Takeaway
A practical parity metric suite has five layers:
1. **Bitwise repeatability:** TARr@N, unique-completions-per-N, divergence-token index.
2. **Answer-level repeatability:** TARa@N, max–min accuracy spread, pass^k.
3. **Semantic equivalence:** canonical-form comparison for JSON and code, bidirectional-entailment clusters / semantic entropy for text.
4. **Invariance:** POSIX, ProSA PSS, flip rate, cross-paraphrase agreement.
5. **Statistical guarantees:** conformal coverage, ECE, MMD two-sample tests for model equality, clustered standard errors.

Each layer catches failures the layer above it misses. For example, GPT-4o reached TARr@10 of 0% while TARa@10 was 50% on one task.

### Cited Findings

**Repeatability metrics**
- (Aug 2024, v5 2 Apr 2025; EVAL4NLP 2025) **TARr@N and TARa@N.** TARr@N is the percentage of questions whose *raw* outputs are string-identical across N runs. TARa@N is the same measure on *parsed answers*.
  - Setup: 5 models (GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4o, Llama-3-70B/8B-Instruct, Mixtral-8x7B) at temperature 0, top-p 1 and fixed seed; 8 BBH and MMLU tasks; 10 runs.
  - Accuracy varied by up to 15% across runs, and the best-to-worst gap reached up to 70%.
  - Mixtral on college math ranged from 75% down to 3%.
  - GPT-4o few-shot on college math: best 88%, median 69%, worst 44%; **TARa@10 = 50%, TARr@10 = 0%**.
  - Output length correlates negatively with TAR and positively with the max–min spread.
  - Recommendations: report max–min accuracy across runs; use regression tests with tolerance; cap max tokens. Per-step instability compounds: four steps at 95% stability give about 81%.

  — [Atil et al., arXiv 2408.04667v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04667v5); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.eval4nlp-1.12)
- (10 Sep 2025) **Unique completions per N and first-divergence token.** Qwen3-235B at temperature 0 gave 1000 completions with **80 unique**. All agreed for the first 102 tokens and diverged at **token 103** ("Queens, New York" 992 times, "New York City" 8 times). Batch-invariant kernels gave 1 unique completion. — [TML](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference)
- (22 Sep 2025) **SGLang determinism test suite.** 50 sampling trials count unique outputs in three subtests:
  - **Single:** the same prompt across batch sizes.
  - **Mixed:** short and long prompts batched together.
  - **Prefix:** shared long prefix at lengths 1, 511, 2048 and 4097.

  Normal mode produced up to **18** unique outputs (FlashInfer, prefix 2048). Deterministic mode produced 1 everywhere. — [LMSYS](https://lmsys.org/blog/2025-09-22-sglang-deterministic/)
- (Jun 2024) **pass^k.** The probability that *all* k i.i.d. trials succeed. τ-bench introduced it to measure agent reliability. It appears as an Inspect reducer citing arXiv 2406.12045. — [τ-bench, arXiv 2406.12045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045); [Inspect metrics docs](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/metrics.html)
- (Jan 2026) Nondeterminism should be measured as shifts in **token probabilities**, not only output strings. Nondeterminism can also be exploited to extract information from providers. — [arXiv 2601.06118](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06118v1)
- (Nov 2023) OpenAI's `seed` gives only best-effort determinism. `system_fingerprint` identifies the backend configuration and changes a few times a year. With the same seed, parameters and fingerprint, outputs are "mostly identical". Azure notes that larger `max_tokens` values give less deterministic outputs. — [OpenAI Cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/reproducible_outputs_with_the_seed_parameter); [Azure docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry-classic/openai/how-to/reproducible-output)

**Prompt sensitivity and invariance metrics**
- (Oct 2024) **POSIX (PrOmpt Sensitivity IndeX).** Measures the length-normalized change in response log-likelihood when the prompt is replaced by an intent-preserving variant. More parameters or instruction tuning do *not* necessarily reduce sensitivity. Cost is O(M·N²) log-likelihood evaluations. — [Chatterjee et al., arXiv 2410.02185](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02185)
- (Oct 2024) **ProSA / PromptSensiScore (PSS).** The average discrepancy across semantic variants of an instruction. 8 LLMs, 4 datasets, 12 variants each. Llama3-70B-Instruct was the most robust, and sensitivity tracks the model's decoding confidence. — [Zhuo et al., arXiv 2410.12405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12405)
- **Flip rate** under challenge prompts was 46% on average (Laban et al., Nov 2023). — [arXiv 2311.08596](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.08596). F²C reports "observed agreement" across prompt variants (Oct 2025). — [arXiv 2510.14242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14242)
- (2020) **CheckList INV / DIR / MFT test types.**
  - **INV:** the prediction must not change under a label-preserving perturbation.
  - **DIR:** the prediction must move in an expected direction.
  - **MFT:** minimum-functionality cases.

  CheckList found significant failures in commercial models from major cloud vendors. — [Ribeiro et al., arXiv 2005.04118](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.04118)

**Semantic equivalence checking**
- (Jun 2024, Nature) **Semantic entropy.** Sample several answers, cluster them by **bidirectional entailment** (A entails B and B entails A, judged by an NLI model or an LLM), and compute entropy over meaning clusters. High semantic entropy flags confabulation. It does not catch systematic, learned errors. — [Farquhar et al., Nature 2024 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11186750)
- (Feb 2025; EMNLP 2025) **EquiBench.** 2,400 program pairs in Python, C, CUDA and x86-64 assembly across 6 categories, with labels from program analysis, compiler scheduling and superoptimization. Across 19 LLMs, the best accuracy in the hardest categories was **63.8% and 76.2%**, against a 50% random baseline, and models rely on syntactic similarity. LLMs are therefore *unreliable as equivalence oracles*. — [arXiv 2502.12466](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12466v3)
- (Jun 2020) **JSON canonicalization (RFC 8785, JCS)** defines a deterministic serialization, which makes byte comparison of semantically equal JSON possible. — [RFC 8785](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785)

**Calibration and conformal guarantees**
- (Jun 2023; ICLR 2024) **Conformal Language Modeling.** A calibrated *stopping rule* keeps sampling until the candidate set contains an acceptable answer with high probability. A second calibrated *rejection rule* prunes low-quality candidates. The guarantees are distribution-free. Evaluated on QA, summarization and radiology report generation. — [Quach et al., arXiv 2306.10193](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10193)
- (Feb 2024; ICML 2024) **Conformal factuality.** The method backs off to less specific claims until a coverage guarantee holds. It achieves **80–90%** correctness guarantees on FActScore, NaturalQuestions and MATH while keeping most of the original output. Limitations: guarantees are *marginal* over calibration and test draws, valid only for the calibration distribution, and inherit the drawbacks of split conformal prediction. — [Mohri & Hashimoto, arXiv 2402.10978](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10978); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mohri24a.html)
- ECE is the standard calibration metric. RLCR and Tian et al. report it, and Tian et al. also report temperature-scaled ECE-t. — [arXiv 2305.14975](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14975); [arXiv 2507.16806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16806)

**Statistical testing for nondeterminism and model identity**
- (Oct 2024; ICLR 2025) **Model Equality Testing.** Framed as a two-sample test between an API and reference weights, using **MMD** with a Hamming-style string kernel. It reaches a median power of **77.4%** with about 10 samples per prompt. Of 31 commercial endpoints serving four Llama models, **11** served a different distribution from Meta's reference weights. A `model-equality-testing` package is released. — [Gao, Liang, Guestrin, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/d73234a13815fc1f9779dd17d89be9b4-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2410.20247](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20247v2)
- (Apr 2025) The same MMD/Hamming approach is used to audit model substitution in LLM APIs. — [arXiv 2504.04715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.04715)
- (Nov 2024) **"Adding Error Bars to Evals" (Anthropic).** Eval questions are treated as drawn from a super-population. The paper gives formulas for analysis, two-model comparison and power planning (sample size), and notes that QA evals rarely report CIs. — [Miller, arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00640)
- (Jun 2025) Recommendation from "Give Me FP32": when compute allows, use random sampling over multiple runs and report mean ± error bars. For single-run greedy decoding, use FP32. — [arXiv 2506.09501](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09501)
- Inspect supports clustered standard errors via `stderr(cluster=...)`. — [Inspect metrics docs](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/metrics.html)

### Inferences
- Proposed "parity scorecard" for a compiler-like LLM system:
  - **(i) Bitwise:** TARr@N with N ≥ 10 across batch sizes, TP sizes and GPU types (the SGLang single/mixed/prefix design), plus median first-divergence token.
  - **(ii) Answer:** TARa@N, max–min spread, pass^k.
  - **(iii) Semantic:** canonical-form match (JCS for JSON; AST/normalized form or execution- or SMT-based equivalence for code; bidirectional-entailment cluster count for text).
  - **(iv) Invariance:** flip rate under a versioned library of metamorphic transformations, plus PSS or POSIX.
  - **(v) Calibration and guarantees:** ECE, conformal coverage at level α.
  - **(vi) Identity across releases and providers:** an MMD two-sample test against a frozen reference.
- EquiBench shows an LLM judge should not be the final arbiter of semantic equivalence for code. Use execution, differential testing or SMT. LLM/NLI entailment is acceptable only for free text, where no formal semantics exists.
- TARr and TARa diverge sharply (0% vs 50% in the GPT-4o example). Parsing and canonicalization layers are therefore themselves a large source of apparent "parity" and must be versioned and tested like compiler front-ends.

### Gaps
- No standard published metric combines bitwise and semantic parity into one certificate. The scorecard above is an inference, not an established standard.
- POSIX and ProSA have not been reported for 2026 frontier models in the sources reviewed.
- No source found that applies conformal prediction specifically to *consistency* (for example, a guarantee that equivalent prompts give equivalent answers with probability ≥ 1−α). Existing work covers correctness and factuality coverage.
- The primary text of the OpenAI hallucination paper (arXiv 2509.04664) was not fetched; only the blog numbers are cited.

## 3. Compiler-trust analogies: which have been applied to LLMs?

### Takeaway
Most compiler-trust techniques now have LLM counterparts as of Oct 2026, but they apply at different layers:
- **Reproducible builds** map to batch/TP/hardware-invariant inference (achieved bitwise in open-source stacks).
- **Differential testing (Csmith)** maps to MMD model-equality tests and inference-engine fuzzing.
- **Metamorphic and property-based testing** are now mainstream for LLMs and LLM-written code.
- **Translation validation and verified code generation (CompCert-like)** apply only where a formal target exists: code with Dafny, Verus or Lean specs, Rust transpilation checked against an oracle, Lean proofs.
- **Runtime guards** (constrained decoding, agent rule DSLs) give hard guarantees only on *form*, not on *content*.

The Csmith/CompCert lesson carries over directly: verification shrinks the trusted base, and testing remains necessary for the unverified parts.

### Cited Findings

**The compiler baseline**
- (PLDI 2011) **Csmith.** Over three years it found **325+** previously unknown bugs in GCC, LLVM and commercial compilers. Every compiler tested crashed *and* silently produced wrong code on valid input. Csmith avoids undefined and unspecified behavior so that differential testing across compilers can detect wrong code. 25 GCC bugs were classified P1 (release-blocking). — [Yang, Chen, Eide, Regehr, PLDI 2011](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~regehr/papers/pldi11-preprint.pdf)
  - On CompCert, the authors write: "the middle-end bugs we found in all other compilers are absent". As of early 2011 it was the only compiler where Csmith found no wrong-code errors, after about **six CPU-years** of testing. Csmith still found 6 wrong-code bugs in CompCert's *unverified front-end*, which led to an expanded verified portion. Their conclusion: "verification does not obviate testing, but rather complements it." — [same](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~regehr/papers/pldi11-preprint.pdf)
- **Reproducible builds.** Given the same source, environment and instructions, anyone can recreate bit-identical artifacts. This is the bitwise-parity standard that batch-invariant inference now meets for LLMs. — [reproducible-builds.org definition](https://reproducible-builds.org/docs/definition/)

**Translation validation and verified generation applied to or with LLMs**
- (Apr 2024; ASE 2025) **VERT.** Source code is compiled to Wasm and then to an rWasm Rust *reference oracle* that is correct by construction but unreadable. An LLM drafts readable Rust, which is checked for equivalence against the oracle by bounded model checking, with retries until it passes. On 1,394 C++/C/Go tasks with Claude-2, **42%** passed bounded verification and **54%** passed differential testing, against **1%** and **31%** for Claude-2 alone. — [Yang et al., arXiv 2404.18852](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18852); [ASE 2025](https://conf.researchr.org/details/ase-2025/ase-2025-papers/57/VERT-Polyglot-Verified-Equivalent-Rust-Transpilation-with-Large-Language-Models)
- (Oct 2025) **RustAssure.** Differential *symbolic* testing for LLM-transpiled C-to-Rust code. — [arXiv 2510.07604](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07604)
- (Jan 2024) **LLM + Alive2 hybrid for LLVM translation validation.** When Alive2 cannot decide, a fine-tuned LLM predicts soundness, and transformations predicted unsound are fuzzed for counterexamples. — [arXiv 2401.16797](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16797v2)
- (Sep 2026) **Trivet** combines LLMs with Lean for LLVM translation validation. It targets cases where Alive2 is limited: bounded SMT, bit-blasting scalability, no symbolic bit widths, bounded loops. — [arXiv 2609.19583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19583)
- (26 Sep 2025) **Vericoding benchmark.** 12,504 formal specs: 3,029 Dafny, 2,334 Verus/Rust, 7,141 Lean; 6,174 are new. Off-the-shelf LLM success rates were **82% Dafny, 44% Verus, 27% Lean**. Pure Dafny verification rose from **68% to 96%** in one year. Adding natural-language descriptions did not help much. — [Bursuc, …, Tegmark, arXiv 2509.22908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22908)
- (Feb 2026; ICML 2026) **AlgoVeri.** 77 classical algorithms with aligned contracts in all three languages. Gemini-3 Flash reached **40.3%** in Dafny; Verus **24.7%**; Lean **7.8%**. Stricter aligned specs explain the lower numbers relative to the vericoding benchmark. — [arXiv 2602.09464](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09464v1)
- (Oct 2025) **Aristotle (Harmonic).** Formal Lean 4 solutions to **5 of 6** IMO 2025 problems, released publicly with no human checking needed because they are machine-verified. — [arXiv 2510.01346](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01346v1); [Harmonic](https://harmonic.fun/news/imo-ios)

**Metamorphic, property-based and differential testing of LLMs**
- (Nov 2025; ICSME 2025) **The most comprehensive metamorphic-testing study of LLMs for NLP.** The authors collected **191** metamorphic relations (MRs), implemented **36**, and ran about **560,000** metamorphic tests on 3 LLMs. — [Cho, Ruberto, Terragni, arXiv 2511.02108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02108)
- (May 2026) A systematic survey of **93** primary studies on the two-way relationship between metamorphic testing and LLMs. — [arXiv 2605.13898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13898)
- (Jun 2025) **Property-Generated Solver.** A PBT-driven generator/tester agent loop gave **23.1–37.3%** relative pass@1 gains over TDD baselines. — [arXiv 2506.18315](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18315v1)
- (ICML 2026) **X-PBT.** Between **2.8% and 15.3%** of programs that pass unit tests on MBPP+ and HumanEval fail an LLM-generated property-based test. — [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/77983)
- (Jun 2025) **PBT on StarCoder and CodeLlama.** 30–32% of solutions only partly satisfy the properties and 18–23% fail outright; property extraction misses 9–13% of constraints. — [VTechWorks](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/702015c7-5db2-4dfc-a098-a8333c50faf6)
- (Jun 2025) **A first look at bugs in LLM inference engines.** 929 real bugs across llama.cpp, vLLM, DeepSpeed, MLC-LLM and TensorRT-LLM; 6 symptom types including unexpected outputs; 28 root causes. — [arXiv 2506.09713](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09713v2)
- (May 2026, aggregator only, unverified) **GRIEF concurrency fuzzing of vLLM and SGLang.** 15 vulnerabilities, 10 confirmed, 2 CVEs, involving cross-request contamination through caching and state reuse. — [chatpaper summary](https://chatpaper.com/chatpaper/paper/279607)
- MMD model-equality testing (Section 2) is the closest LLM analogue to Csmith-style *differential* testing across implementations of the "same" model. — [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/d73234a13815fc1f9779dd17d89be9b4-Abstract-Conference.html)

**Spec-driven development and runtime verification / guards**
- (Sep 2025) **GitHub Spec Kit.** Spec → Plan → Tasks → Implement, where each phase produces a Markdown artifact that feeds the next. MIT-licensed; works with Copilot, Claude Code and Gemini CLI. — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/spec-driven-development-with-ai-get-started-with-a-new-open-source-toolkit/); [Visual Studio Magazine 2025-09-03](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/articles/2025/09/03/github-open-sources-kit-for-spec-driven-ai-development.aspx). A secondary review reports **115K+ stars by June 2026**. — [ssojet](https://ssojet.com/blog/best-spec-driven-development-tools)
- (Mar 2025; ICSE 2026) **AgentSpec.** A DSL of triggers, predicates and enforcement actions for runtime enforcement on LLM agents. Reported results: unsafe executions prevented in >90% of code-agent cases, all hazardous embodied-agent actions eliminated, 100% compliance for autonomous vehicles, with millisecond overhead. — [Wang, Poskitt, Sun, arXiv 2503.18666](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18666)
- (Aug 2024; secondary sources) **Constrained decoding as a hard guard.** OpenAI's internal complex JSON-schema eval: GPT-4-0613 <40% adherence; GPT-4o-2024-08-06 93% without Structured Outputs and **100%** with `strict: true`. The mechanism is a schema compiled to a CFG or FSM that masks tokens. It guarantees *shape*, not correct values. — [futureagi](https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-llm-structured-output-modes-2026/); [morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/structured-output-llm)

### Inferences
- The CompCert pattern maps onto LLM systems as "LLM proposes, checker disposes". The LLM is an untrusted optimizer or translator; a small trusted checker (Dafny, Verus or Lean kernel; bounded model checker against an oracle as in VERT; schema FSM) certifies each output. This is *translation validation per output*, not verification of the model. Because the model is not trusted, it is the only route to compiler-grade guarantees that does not depend on model determinism.
- Trust layers for an "LLM compiler", from strongest to weakest:
  1. Machine-checked proof against a formal spec (vericoding, Lean).
  2. Equivalence checking against a reference oracle (VERT, Alive2-style).
  3. Property-based and metamorphic testing (statistical, no oracle needed).
  4. Differential testing across models, engines or releases (MMD).
  5. Runtime guards on form (grammars, AgentSpec).
- Csmith's six CPU-years against CompCert suggests a concrete certification protocol: keep fuzzing the verified pipeline with randomized metamorphic and differential inputs. Bugs will collect in the unverified "front-end": prompt parsing, tool glue, tokenization, the serving engine (as the 929-bug inference-engine study shows).
- Verified-code success varies a lot by target language (Dafny 82% vs Lean 27% on vericoding; 40% vs 8% on AlgoVeri). Target-language choice is therefore a design lever for any verified-generation pipeline.

### Gaps
- No LLM-specific Csmith analogue was found: a random generator of semantically well-defined prompts whose correct output is known by construction, used to differentially test models or engines at scale.
- No end-to-end "CompCert for LLMs" exists. No work verifies the model itself; all formal work verifies individual outputs.
- The primary OpenAI Structured Outputs announcement was not fetched; the numbers come from secondary blogs.
- GRIEF was seen only through an aggregator. The primary paper and its methodology are unverified.
- Hacker News and Kaggle discussion was not surveyed; there was no time budget for it.

## 4. Evaluation infrastructure: how do harnesses and labs test release-to-release behavioral parity?

### Takeaway
The open harnesses (lm-evaluation-harness, Inspect, HELM, promptfoo, OpenAI evals) all support seeding, logging and repeated-epoch scoring, but none *guarantees* bitwise reproducibility, which depends on the serving numerics. Release-to-release parity is still the weakest link:
- Published incidents (the GPT-4o sycophancy rollback, Anthropic's August 2025 infrastructure bugs) show that offline evals and A/B tests missed behavioral regressions.
- Academic work on "negative flips" (MUSCLE) and LLM migration regression testing (RETAIN) is the main formal treatment.

### Cited Findings

**Harnesses**
- (May 2024) **"Lessons from the Trenches on Reproducible Evaluation of LMs" (EleutherAI).** The paper documents sensitivity to evaluation setup, difficulty comparing across methods, and poor reproducibility. It presents lm-eval as the reference tool. — [Biderman et al., arXiv 2405.14782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.14782)
- **lm-eval `--seed`.** The default is `0,1234,1234,1234` for python, numpy, torch and few-shot. `--log_samples` saves all inputs and outputs, and `--use_cache` keeps a SQLite cache of responses. The docs make *no* claim of bit-identical GPU or backend results. — [lm-eval interface docs](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/blob/main/docs/interface.md)
- **Inspect AI (UK AISI).**
  - `Epochs(n, reducer)` runs repeated trials. Built-in reducers: `mean`, `median`, `mode`, `majority` (>50% else unscored), `max`, `pass_at_{k}`, `pass_k_{k}` (all k succeed), `at_least_{k}` and `collect`.
  - `answer` is retained only if it matches across all epochs.
  - Clustered standard errors via `stderr(cluster=...)`.

  — [Inspect Scoring Metrics docs](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/metrics.html); [multiple scorers](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/multiple-scorers.html)
  - Version 0.3.268 was on PyPI as of 22 Sep 2026 (secondary source). It is MIT-licensed and co-developed with Meridian Labs, with K8s, EC2, Proxmox, Modal and Daytona sandboxes. — [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/inspect-ai-evaluation-framework-uk-aisi); [inspect.aisi.org.uk](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk)
- (Nov 2022) **HELM** evaluates many metrics per scenario, including calibration and robustness alongside accuracy, which makes it a natural host for consistency metrics. — [Liang et al., arXiv 2211.09110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110)
- **promptfoo.** An open-source eval and red-team tool with CI integration that runs tests on every PR or on a schedule, covering 50+ vulnerability types. OpenAI announced its acquisition on **9 Mar 2026** and said promptfoo would stay open source. — [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-acquires-promptfoo-ai-security-frontier); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/openai-acquires-promptfoo-secure-ai-174904138.html)
- **OpenAI evals** is the open-source eval registry. — [github.com/openai/evals](https://github.com/openai/evals)
- (Mar 2024) Hamel Husain: unsuccessful AI products almost always lack robust evals. LLM evals differ from unit tests in that "you don't necessarily need a 100% pass rate. Your pass rate is a product decision." — [Simon Willison on Husain](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Mar/31/your-ai-product-needs-evals)

**Release-to-release parity and behavioral regression**
- (Jul 2024) **MUSCLE (Apple).** Base-model updates cause **negative flips** (instances that the old model got right and the new one gets wrong) in downstream task adapters, even with identical fine-tuning. A compatibility adapter cut negative flips by up to **40%** (Llama 1 to Llama 2), but only **1–5%** for large capability jumps (Phi 1 to Phi 1.5). — [arXiv 2407.09435](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09435v2)
- (Nov 2023; CAIN 2024) **Silent API updates.** gpt-3.5-turbo was updated twice by Nov 2023 without developers being able to see it. Upgrades can significantly degrade performance, the prompt is a key factor in behavior change, and regression tests must account for nondeterminism (flakiness). — [Ma, Yang, Kästner, arXiv 2311.11123](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.11123v2)
- (Sep 2024; EMNLP 2024 demo) **RETAIN.** An interactive tool for regression testing during LLM migration (re-engineering prompts to keep behavior consistent across models). Users found **2x** as many errors as with manual evaluation. — [arXiv 2409.03928](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.03928)
- (2 May 2025) **OpenAI GPT-4o sycophancy postmortem.** Offline evals, including behavior tests, "generally looked good" and A/B tests were positive, but sycophancy was neither a focus of hands-on testing nor tracked in deployment checks. OpenAI is adding sycophancy evals to its release process. Simon Willison's critique: automated evals and A/B tests overruled the vibe checks. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/2/what-we-missed-with-sycophancy/)
- (17 Sep 2025) **Anthropic postmortem.** Three infrastructure bugs caused the quality degradation:
  - A context-window routing error that affected **16%** of Sonnet 4 requests at the 31 Aug peak.
  - A TPU misconfiguration that produced Thai and Chinese characters in English output.
  - A third bug that, per The Decoder's summary, made the system select lower-quality outputs more often than it should. The primary postmortem text was not fetched.

  Internal checks did not catch these quickly; detection relied on community reports, and privacy controls limited inspection. Anthropic committed to more sensitive evals and continuous monitoring. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-explains-recent-claude-quality-drop-three-technical-failures-to-blame/); [Simon Willison](https://feeds.simonwillison.net/2025/Sep/17/anthropic-postmortem/); [Claude status incident](https://status.claude.com/incidents/72f99lh1cj2c)
- (Oct 2024; ICLR 2025) 11 of 31 hosted Llama endpoints were distributionally different from the reference weights. This shows that "same model name" does not imply parity across providers. — [Model Equality Testing](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20247v2)
- (Oct 2025; ICSE 2026) **Reproducibility of published LLM-based SE research.** Of 18 ICSE/ASE 2024 studies using OpenAI models, only 5 were complete and executable, and **none** of those 5 could be fully reproduced (2 partly, 3 not at all). — [arXiv 2510.25506](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25506v3)

### Inferences
- A release gate for "compiler-grade" parity should combine:
  1. A frozen golden set with TARr/TARa repeatability under deterministic serving.
  2. A **negative-flip budget** per capability between versions (MUSCLE's metric) rather than only aggregate accuracy.
  3. An MMD equality test of new against old on a canary prompt set, to detect *unintended* distribution shift such as quantization or a serving change.
  4. Metamorphic and invariance suites (CheckList INV/DIR, BCT transformation library).
  5. Targeted behavior evals for known failure classes (sycophancy, language drift).
  6. Production canaries, because both lab postmortems show offline evals miss serving-layer regressions.
- Inspect's `pass_k`, `majority` and per-epoch `answer` agreement can implement TARa@N and pass^k directly. lm-eval's `--log_samples` and `--use_cache` give the artifacts needed for TARr diffs.
- The `system_fingerprint` mechanism is a weak, provider-controlled form of a "build ID". A compiler-like ecosystem needs a stronger, user-verifiable equivalent: weights hash, kernel and numerics config hash, and a sampler seed.

### Gaps
- No public, detailed description was found of how OpenAI, Anthropic or Google DeepMind run *release-to-release behavioral diff* suites: negative-flip budgets, MMD tests, golden sets. Only postmortem-level descriptions are available.
- Eugene Yan and other practitioner blog guidance on golden datasets was not retrieved in this pass.
- HELM's current (2026) support for repeated-run consistency metrics was not verified.

## 5. Fundamental limits: where does full determinism conflict with capability, and when is "semantic determinism" the right target?

### Takeaway
Bitwise determinism for a *fixed* model, input and configuration is now an engineering problem with known solutions and costs: about 1.3–1.6x slowdown, now extended across TP sizes and GPU architectures. It is not a fundamental limit. Three real limits remain:
- **Ambiguity:** the input does not determine a unique correct output.
- **The entropy–capability coupling:** collapsing entropy caps exploration, pass@k and diversity.
- **Undecidable or expensive equivalence:** semantic equivalence of programs is hard even for LLMs, and conformal guarantees are only marginal.

The defensible target is therefore *semantic determinism with calibrated abstention*: equivalent inputs give equivalent outputs, ambiguous inputs give clarification or abstention, and outputs are verified against a spec where one exists. Raw bitwise identity is used for reproducibility and debugging, not as the trust criterion.

### Cited Findings
- (Apr 2025) Atil et al. argued that nondeterminism may be "essential to the efficient use of compute resources" through co-mingled batches, and expected it to persist. — [arXiv 2408.04667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667). Later engineering work showed it can be removed at a measured cost:
  - TML: 26 s to 42 s. — [TML, Sep 2025](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference)
  - SGLang: about 34% average overhead. — [LMSYS, Sep 2025](https://lmsys.org/blog/2025-09-22-sglang-deterministic/)
  - Cross-TP: TBIK. — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66224)
  - Cross-GPU-architecture: ReproFuse. — [arXiv 2609.25624, Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25624)
- (12 Jan 2026, v2 11 Oct 2026) **"Stochastic CHAOS: Why Deterministic Inference Kills…".** The authors argue that LLMs are conditional distributions and that bitwise determinism is a misleading imported software ideal. Their qualitative claims:
  - It hides uncertainty and tail risks.
  - Single-sample deterministic evaluation underestimates both capability and fragility under paraphrase.
  - Emergent-ability transitions vanish under greedy decoding.
  - Multi-path reasoning loses accuracy on deterministic backbones.
  - Rare dangerous behaviors surface only under multi-sample evaluation.

  The abstract gives no numbers. — [Joshi et al., arXiv 2601.07239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07239)
- **Entropy–capability coupling:**
  - R = −a·e^H + b: performance is bounded as entropy goes to 0. — [Cui et al., May 2025](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.22617)
  - RLVR narrows pass@k coverage. — [Yue et al., Apr 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13837)
  - Partial counter-evidence: ProRL. — [May 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24864)
  - RLHF trades diversity for generalization. — [Kirk et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06452v3)
- (Oct 2025; NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper) **"Artificial Hivemind".** Infinity-Chat has 26K real open-ended queries and 31,250 human annotations (25 per example). Models repeat themselves (intra-model) and different models give strikingly similar outputs (inter-model homogeneity). Reward models and LM judges are poorly calibrated to idiosyncratic human preferences. — [Jiang et al., arXiv 2510.22954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954v1); [NeurIPS 2025 awards](https://blog.neurips.cc/2025/11/26/announcing-the-neurips-2025-best-paper-awards/)
- (Oct 2025) Mode collapse comes partly from typicality bias in human preference data. Prompting for a *distribution* (verbalized sampling) recovers diversity without training. — [arXiv 2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171v1)
- (Apr 2023; EMNLP 2023) **AmbiEnt** has 1,645 linguist-annotated ambiguous examples. GPT-4's disambiguations were judged correct only **32%** of the time, against **90%** for the human-written ones. Ambiguous instances are systematically excluded when benchmarks are curated. — [Liu et al., arXiv 2304.14399](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.14399)
- (Sep 2025) Accuracy-only grading incentivizes guessing over abstaining. Abstention-aware scoring changes which model looks best: GPT-5-thinking-mini had 52% abstention and 26% error, against 1% and 75% for o4-mini. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/)
- (Jun 2024) Semantic entropy formalizes *meaning-level* variance, separating harmless paraphrase variance from variance in what is asserted. That distinction is what "semantic determinism" requires. — [Farquhar et al., Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11186750)
- (Feb 2025) Judging program equivalence is hard for LLMs: at best 63.8% and 76.2% in the hardest EquiBench categories, against a 50% baseline. Semantic-parity checking for code must rely on execution or formal tools. — [EquiBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12466v3)
- (Feb 2024) Conformal guarantees are marginal and valid only for the calibration distribution. They are not per-instance certificates. — [Mohri & Hashimoto](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10978)
- (Jun 2025) Even with greedy decoding, BF16 numerics cause up to 9% accuracy variation in reasoning models. Long chains of thought amplify early rounding differences into divergent reasoning. — [arXiv 2506.09501](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09501). Output length correlates with instability. — [Atil et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04667v5)

### Inferences
- Use a three-level target hierarchy:
  1. **Bitwise determinism** of the *system* (weights, kernels, sampler seed) for reproducibility, auditability, debugging and true on-policy RL. It is cheap enough to require for regulated or "compiler-mode" endpoints.
  2. **Semantic determinism** of the *function*: equivalent inputs give outputs in the same meaning class, measured by canonical-form equality, entailment clusters or execution equivalence. This is the actual trust criterion.
  3. **Spec satisfaction** of *outputs*, by verifier or guard, where a spec exists.

  Creativity and exploration modes should remain stochastic and be evaluated *distributionally* (pass@k, conformal sets, multi-sample risk). Stochastic CHAOS's critique applies to using bitwise determinism as an *evaluation* method, not as a serving option.
- Ambiguity sets a floor on achievable semantic determinism. For an ambiguous input, a consistent system should deterministically choose *to ask or abstain* rather than collapse to an arbitrary reading, which links consistency to clarification and abstention training (Section 1F).
- Because entropy minimization raises pass@1 consistency but can lower pass@k and diversity, a "compiler mode" checkpoint is a deliberately specialized artifact. Its domain should be constrained to tasks with checkable specs, where low entropy plus verification is safe.
- Rice's theorem (general undecidability of semantic program properties) means "semantic parity" for code can only be checked soundly over bounded inputs (testing, bounded model checking) or with proofs against explicit specs. This is consistent with the VERT, vericoding and Alive2 approaches above. This is an inference from standard computability theory, not from a cited LLM paper.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found that measures the *creativity or diversity cost* of bitwise-deterministic serving itself, as distinct from greedy decoding or low-entropy training. With seeded sampling (SGLang's Gumbel sampler), determinism and diversity are in principle orthogonal, but this has not been empirically characterized.
- Stochastic CHAOS's empirical claims could not be checked numerically from the abstract.
- No formal paper found that defines "semantic determinism" as a named metric or standard. The term is used informally, and the definition above is synthesized.
- No standard or certification scheme for LLM output parity was found (comparable to DO-178C or ISO 26262 tool qualification). Certification remains a research gap as of Oct 2026.
