# Compiler-Grade LLMs Need Contracts, Not Temperature Zero

As of October 2026, every component of a deterministic "model compiler" exists in open source, but nobody has put them together, and the hard part has moved from GPU arithmetic to the natural-language front-end. Getting byte-identical output for the same prompt on a pinned stack is now a configuration choice rather than a research problem: vLLM's `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1` lists DeepSeek-V3/R1, Qwen3-30B-A3B and gpt-oss among its tested MoEs, and a co-designed DeepSeek-V4-Flash build held train–inference divergence at exactly zero for 100 RL steps at **91.3% of baseline throughput** ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-14-train-inference-consistency)). MoE routing adds its own fragility — **about 10% of router decisions and 94% of tokens differ** between two engines running Qwen3-30B-A3B ([R3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11370v2)) — which an FP32 router, stable top-k tie-breaks, dropless routing, fixed-order expert combines and logged routing paths close. Above the numerics, compiler-like parity has to be built rather than decoded: the model emits a closed, typed DSL under grammar-constrained decoding, that program is canonicalized, hashed, executed by deterministic code and checked by verifiers, and specialists sit behind a static task-type routing table that 2026 benchmarks show matches or beats most learned routers ([Lee](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23023)). Deep discovery of a Qwen-class MoE can be assembled from Qwen-Scope SAEs, circuit-tracer, nnsight and MoE-specific tools, but no lab has published a full layer, expert and router map of a 30B MoE, and interventions reliably move discrete decisions without any published evidence that they reduce sampling variance. The defensible target is a three-level contract — bitwise reproducibility on a pinned hardware/software "target triple", semantic determinism measured as paraphrase agreement, and spec verification on each output — with ambiguous inputs routed to clarification instead of guessed. One correction to the premise: Qwen3.8-27B is a dense model; the Qwen-class MoE candidates are Qwen3-30B-A3B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and the larger Qwen3.5/3.8 MoEs ([Qwen3.8-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)).

## Batch invariance, not GPU randomness, breaks temperature-zero parity

The standard demonstration ran on an MoE. Thinking Machines Lab sent the same prompt to Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 1,000 times at temperature 0 and got **80 unique completions**; all of them agreed for 102 tokens, then split at token 103 into "Queens, New York" (992) and "New York City" (8) ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)). The cause was not atomics or "GPU concurrency" — a single forward pass gives the same bits every time it runs — but a **lack of batch invariance**. Server load changes the batch size, RMSNorm, matmul (split-K) and attention (split-KV) kernels choose different reduction strategies for different batch sizes, and because floating-point addition is not associative, the same request produces different bits. The fix makes reduction order independent of the batch: data-parallel RMSNorm, no split-K, and a fixed split *size* rather than a fixed split count in decode attention. With it, all 1,000 completions matched; Qwen3-8B runtime rose from 26 s to 42 s (55 s before the attention kernel was optimized), and an RL run whose sampler and trainer were bitwise identical held KL divergence at exactly zero.

Batch size is only the most visible variable. Vosti (Sep 2026) defines the target as bitwise-identical logits regardless of batch composition, chunked prefill, the prefill-versus-decode path and KV-cache reuse, then shows that shipped "deterministic modes" still leak on the last three: SGLang's Triton backend matched only **3 of 384** prefill–decode pairs on Llama-3.1-8B, and all four production deterministic configurations mismatched in every category on sliding-window Gemma3-4B ([Vosti](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38981v1)). Vosti's own stack, whose scheduler and paged prefix cache are proven in Verus and whose kernels are proven invariant by a Triton analyzer, passed **5,488 of 5,488** variation pairs. A TensorRT-LLM RFC finds eight batch-dependent sources in that engine alone, including GEMM autotuner tactics, MoE grouped-GEMM tactic selection by token count, a non-deterministic MoE finalize/unpermute kernel, all-reduce strategy chosen by message size and the CUDA-graph padding ladder ([TRT-LLM RFC #19501](https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/issues/19501)). In one cross-GPU study a date string inside a chat template was enough to cause spurious divergence ([arXiv 2609.25624](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25624v1)). Determinism is a property of the whole system, not of individual kernels.

The stakes rise with output length. Under BF16 greedy decoding, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B showed a **9.15% standard deviation in AIME'24 accuracy** and response-length differences of roughly 9,000 tokens across GPU type, GPU count and batch size — all zero in FP32 — and the mean first divergent token moved from about 65 in BF16 to 395 in FP16 and 1,826 in FP32, tracking mantissa width ([LayerCast](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09501v2)). Individual flips are rare, **0.3–1.3% of decode steps**, and concentrate on tokens with a small margin between the top two logits ([MarginGate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30218)), but once a sequence diverges it stays divergent ([LLM-42](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17768v2)). Long reasoning and agent traces without invariant numerics will almost certainly diverge.

Engine support has matured unevenly. vLLM is furthest along: `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1`, still labelled beta, runs on NVIDIA, AMD Instinct, Intel XPU and Ascend, lists DeepSeek-V3/R1/V3.1, Qwen3-30B-A3B, Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B, gpt-oss-20b/120b and Granite MoE as tested, and keeps tensor parallelism deterministic with a pinned-order custom all-reduce ([vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/batch_invariance/)). Its tracking issue records a deterministic MoE combine, DP=2×TP=2 with expert parallelism validated on GB300, green AMD CI with expert parallelism, and bitwise-equal MTP speculative decoding on B300 — but no stated prefix-caching support, and a regression that stopped Qwen3-Next from starting in batch-invariant mode as of Oct 4, 2026 ([vLLM #27433](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/27433)); doc versions also disagree on the minimum compute capability (8.0 vs 9.0). SGLang's `--enable-deterministic-inference` supports FA3, FlashInfer and Triton backends plus seeded Gumbel sampling, so a given seed reproduces output at temperature above zero, and documents a Qwen3-30B-A3B example, but expert parallelism was still pending when its roadmap closed ([SGLang docs](https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/deterministic_inference.html); [SGLang #10278](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/10278)). TensorRT-LLM has only a partial `FORCE_DETERMINISTIC` — which silently ignores `=true` because only the string "1" matches — and a community RFC for a full mode ([TRT-LLM RFC #19501](https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/issues/19501)). The research found no batch-invariant serving mode in llama.cpp, Hugging Face TGI/transformers or JAX/TPU.

Cost depends on whether invariance is bolted on or designed in, and a second family of designs avoids charging every request for it:

| Approach (date) | How it achieves determinism | Measured cost | Validated scope |
|---|---|---|---|

| vLLM with DeepGEMM batch-invariant mode (Oct 2026) | Batch-invariant dense and expert GEMMs, FP32 router, deterministic top-k, FP32 expert merge in slot order | 91.3% of baseline rollout throughput | DeepSeek-V4-Flash, DP8/EP8, TP=1, 32 GB200 GPUs |
| TBIK (ICML 2026) | One hierarchical reduction tree within and across GPUs | +22–63% over BF16 | Same output at every tensor-parallel size; MoE untested |
| LLM-42 (Jan 2026) | Fast path, then a fixed-shape verifier that rolls back mismatches | Within 3% of non-deterministic serving when 1 in 11 requests needs determinism | Llama-3.1-8B; correctness only on Qwen3-30B-A3B |

Two conclusions follow. Generic batch-invariant kernels cost roughly 20–60%, and server-wide modes charge every request: mixing one deterministic request into ten others cut SGLang throughput by about 56%, and the batch-invariant GEMM reached 194 TFLOPS against cuBLAS's 527 ([LLM-42](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17768v2)). Yet the vLLM DeepSeek-V4-Flash work shows the gap is kernel engineering, not physics — on a pure inference benchmark, after matching the data-transport path, its consistent build ran 3.2% *faster* than baseline ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-14-train-inference-consistency)). A model compiler should therefore sell determinism as a per-request service level, falling back to verify-and-rollback (LLM-42, MarginGate) or padding (CoRun) where only part of the traffic needs it.

Cross-hardware parity is the remaining frontier, and it sets the scope of any guarantee. Same-GPU-model reproducibility is solved: EigenAI matched 100% across hosts with the same GPU but **0% between A100 and H100**, and traced some leftover nondeterminism to faulty memory that enabling ECC fixed ([EigenAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00182v1)); cuBLAS itself promises bitwise reproducibility only on the same architecture and SM count within one toolkit version ([cuBLAS docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cublas/index.html#results-reproducibility)). Across NVIDIA generations, fixed-configuration GEMMs that upcast BF16 to FP32 in registers made linear-layer outputs bitwise identical on A100, L40S and H100 and drove cross-GPU greedy divergence to 0% in 11 of 12 cells, with a 0.08% residual traced to unpinned H100 attention; AMD, Blackwell and MoE remain uncovered ([arXiv 2609.25624](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25624v1)). Integer arithmetic is the most promising route across different kernels: rounding every INT8 weight scale to a power of two made CUTLASS and Triton agree bitwise at all 196 linear layers of Qwen3 and produced byte-identical generations (8 of 8 prompts, versus 0 of 8) with under 0.5% perplexity change — and the same study found that conformance checks with a one-BF16-ULP tolerance missed four of five classes of injected epilogue faults ([arXiv 2609.00363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00363)). Combined with batch-invariant ops, TBIK makes output identical across tensor-parallel sizes, cutting unique AIME outputs on Qwen3-8B from 12 to 1 ([TBIK](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17826)). No published result shows bitwise LLM inference across vendors. The practical contract is therefore tiered: Tier A is bitwise on a pinned target triple of GPU model, driver, container digest, kernel set and parallel layout; Tier B is bitwise across NVIDIA generations, which requires owning every kernel; Tier C crosses vendors and requires integer or correctly rounded arithmetic at unknown cost.

RL post-training, not chat, is the main commercial driver, and it pays a free dividend to a compiler. Rollout engines and trainers compute different log-probabilities for the same weights, silently turning on-policy RL into off-policy RL; in the vLLM DeepSeek-V4-Flash run the baseline's train–inference KL climbed from about 2×10⁻³ to **29.9** while reward fell back, whereas the consistent build stayed at zero ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-14-train-inference-consistency)). Cheaper partial fixes exist — switching from BF16 to FP16 ([arXiv 2510.26788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26788)), importance-sampling corrections, routing replay — but a serving stack that shares its kernels with the training forward pass gets bitwise train–inference parity almost for free.

## MoE routers turn rounding noise into different experts

Router definitions are not the problem. Every major open MoE shipped in 2025–2026 uses dropless, per-token, token-choice top-k routing: softmax in Qwen3 through Qwen3.8, sigmoid in DeepSeek-V3, and sqrt(softplus) with a bias-based balancer instead of an auxiliary loss in DeepSeek-V4, whose first three MoE layers route by a fixed hash of the token ID ([DeepSeek-V4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19348v1); [Qwen3 TR](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388)). Designs that depend on the batch by construction — expert-choice routing, where experts pick tokens from the batch ([arXiv 2202.09368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09368)), capacity-factor token dropping, BASE balanced assignment and noisy gating — are confined to training or abandoned. In principle each token's expert set depends only on its own hidden state; in practice a discrete top-k sits downstream of every rounding difference.

How often that bites is now measured. Replaying 2,048 SGLang rollouts of Qwen3-30B-A3B through Megatron, the R3 authors found that **about 10% of router decisions differed and 94% of tokens chose a different expert in at least one layer**, about six routers per token on average; the MoE's train–inference KL (1.5×10⁻³) was 2.4 times that of dense Qwen3-8B, and even two Megatron passes over identical sequences disagreed (KL 8.4×10⁻⁴) ([R3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11370v2)). Precision matters most at the router itself: an FP8 router produced the largest rollout/train mismatch, a BF16 router was sufficient, FP32 added only marginal benefit, and MoE mismatch grew over training where dense mismatch did not ([NVIDIA FP8-RL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18150)). The behavioral cost lands where it hurts most. Sampling 32 alternative routes showed **5.5–14.8% of tokens are "route-fragile"** across Qwen3-30B-A3B, gpt-oss-20b, DeepSeek-V2-Lite and OLMoE, more often on AIME (10.5%) than GPQA (5.5%); on those tokens the best alternative route raised next-token probability by about 20 points, and the effect grew with depth ([arXiv 2605.07260](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07260)). In an MoE, a numerical flip at a fragile token changes behavior, and fragile tokens cluster in hard reasoning. No production MoE has a published per-layer router-margin distribution; measuring one is a first-order task for this program.

MoE serving adds batch-dependent code paths that dense-model fixes miss. In September 2026 a vLLM issue showed that `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1` was **not** batch-invariant for fused-MoE experts with online FP8, because one dynamic activation scale was computed over the whole batch: granite-3.1-1b-a400m flipped at the very first token on H100 depending on co-batched requests, and BF16 Triton experts chose tile configurations by token count, so requests that hit the prefix cache diverged from cold ones ([vLLM #57016](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/57016)). AMD's aiter added a deterministic fused-MoE mode because down-projection atomic adds complete in workgroup order ([aiter #6583](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/6583)); TensorRT-LLM's CUTLASS finalize is non-deterministic for top-k above two ([TRT-LLM RFC #19501](https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/issues/19501)); and vLLM's draft DeepSeek-V4 batch-invariant PR replaces the variable expert capacity (`expert_num_tokens.max()`) with a fixed one, computes gate logits with a single FP32 matmul and adds a stable top-k tie-break ([vLLM #54955](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/54955)). DeepSeek-V4 itself orders tokens per rank and isolates buffers so that expert-parallel sends and MoE backward accumulation are deterministic, aiming at bitwise alignment across pre-training, post-training and inference ([DeepSeek-V4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19348v1)), while vLLM's consistent V4-Flash build merges expert outputs locally in FP32 in top-k slot order and breaks sparse-attention top-k ties by position for about 5.9 µs per call ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-14-train-inference-consistency)).

Read together, these sources define a deterministic-MoE serving recipe. Router logits are computed in FP32 through one fixed GEMM and top-k breaks ties by lowest expert index; routing is dropless, or uses a fixed capacity that cannot overflow; activation quantization scales are per token, never per batch; the grouped GEMM uses one tile configuration regardless of tokens per expert, with no split-K or atomics in the down-projection or combine; expert outputs are combined in FP32 in a fixed slot order; expert-parallel all-to-all moves data but never reduces it; and the TP/EP topology is frozen, since no engine claims MoE invariance across different EP or TP degrees. The vLLM #57016 reproducer (co-batch and warm-versus-cold prefix flips on granite-1b-a400m) and SGLang's `test_deterministic` single, prefix and radix-cache modes are ready-made regression tests. Hybrid linear-attention models such as Qwen3.5 and later add Gated DeltaNet recurrent kernels to the invariance surface, and an SGLang issue reports that its flag did not make hybrid Mamba2 Granite batch-invariant ([SGLang #43413](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/43413)).

Beyond making routing reproducible, a compiler can make routing *matter less*. Routing replay turns expert choice into a recorded artifact: R3 logs top-k masks per token at under 3% rollout overhead and caches them alongside the KV prefix ([R3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11370v2)), and for a 48-layer, top-8 model the expert IDs and weights fit in roughly 1–2 KB per token. Hash routing removes numerics from the decision entirely; it was competitive with learned routers in the original Hash Layers work ([arXiv 2106.04426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04426)) and now runs in DeepSeek-V4's early layers. StableMoE distills and then freezes a router so its assignments stop fluctuating ([arXiv 2204.08396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08396)). Task-level control is the most interesting lever. ESFT found that "the routing distribution for a specific task tends to be highly concentrated" while activated experts vary across tasks, and that tuning only task-relevant experts matched full fine-tuning ([ESFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01906)); REAP pruned 50% of experts in Qwen3-Coder-480B and Kimi-K2 with near-zero loss on code and showed pruning beats merging because merging destroys routing control ([REAP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13999)); router-only LoRA on about 0.002% of parameters let a half-pruned Mixtral keep 27.54% MMLU-Pro versus about 16% for magnitude pruning ([arXiv 2608.07890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07890)). The logical "deterministic MoE" is therefore a per-task expert allow-list applied as a top-k mask, with separate profiles for decision, function-calling, judging, creative and reasoning work. No study has measured what task-masked routing costs in quality on a 2026 model, which makes it the single most valuable experiment for this project. Fine-grained MoEs trained from scratch are the right substrate: OLMoE showed strong domain specialization while Mixtral, upcycled from a dense model, was near-uniform ([OLMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02060v2)), and the global-batch load balancing Qwen3 adopted "greatly improves domain specialization" ([arXiv 2501.11873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11873)).

## A full circuit map of a 30B MoE is buildable but unpublished

The 2026 pipeline has three steps: gradient-based attribution patching localizes the components that matter, sparse dictionaries name features, and attribution graphs propose circuits that interventions must confirm. Edge attribution patching scores every edge with two forward passes and one backward pass and beats ACDC ([EAP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10348)); EAP-IG corrects its linear-approximation errors ([EAP-IG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17806)); the MIB benchmark found attribution and mask-optimization methods best at circuit localization ([MIB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13151)). Anthropic's cross-layer transcoder (CLT) replaces all MLPs with a sparse "replacement model", but at 30M features on Claude 3.5 Haiku normalized reconstruction error was still 21.7%, and attribution graphs gave "satisfying insight for about a quarter of the prompts" ([Circuit Tracing](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html); [Biology](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html)). Graphs generate hypotheses; patching, ablation and steering are the acceptance test.

The assets for a Qwen-class MoE are better than for any other open MoE. Qwen-Scope (May 12, 2026) released residual-stream SAEs for every layer of Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base (48 layers) and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base (40 layers), each in a 32K-feature (TopK k=50) and a 128K-feature (k=100) width, though without training-compute or reconstruction-quality figures ([Qwen-Scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11887v1)). circuit-tracer, open-sourced by Anthropic in May 2025, supports a 131K-feature CLT for GPT-OSS-20B — the only public MoE replacement model — alongside per-layer transcoders for dense Qwen3 up to 14B, with an nnsight backend still marked experimental ([circuit-tracer](https://github.com/safety-research/circuit-tracer)). Neuronpedia hosts gpt-oss-20b SAEs but has no Qwen3-30B-A3B page ([Neuronpedia](https://www.neuronpedia.org/gpt-oss-20b)), and Goodfire trained DeepSeek-R1 SAEs at a single layer on custom infrastructure ([Goodfire](https://www.goodfire.com/research/under-the-hood-of-a-reasoning-model)). Around these sit mature tools: nnsight/NDIF runs the same intervention code locally or remotely on models up to 405B+ ([nnsight](https://nnsight.net/features/13_remote_execution/)), TransformerLens 3.x's TransformerBridge ships `qwen3_moe`, `qwen2_moe` and `olmoe` adapters ([TransformerLens](https://github.com/TransformerLensOrg/TransformerLens)), CLT-Forge trains CLTs on distributed hardware with circuit-tracer integration ([CLT-Forge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21014)), and MoE Lens decodes each expert's residual contribution ([MoE Lens](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05806v1)). Two MoE-specific problems remain. TransformerLens warns that small numerical errors are "amplified greatly" when a different expert is chosen ([TransformerLens docs](https://transformerlensorg.github.io/TransformerLens/content/special_cases.html)), so discovery must start with determinism itself; and a CLT that replaces a whole MoE block absorbs the router's top-k nonlinearity, so feature graphs lose track of which expert ran unless routing is logged separately. No library yet treats experts as attribution-graph nodes.

What MoE interpretability has found reshapes what "deep discovery" should look for. A handful of early-layer **super experts** create massive activations and attention sinks: Qwen3-30B-A3B has three of its 6,144 routed experts in this class (layer 1 expert 68, layer 2 expert 92, layer 3 expert 82), and pruning them dropped thinking-mode Math-500 from **88.0 to 1.4**, drove AIME to zero and produced repetitive text, while pruning as many random experts did nothing measurable ([Super Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279v2)). Behavior-linked experts respond to router-logit edits: SteerMoE forces chosen experts on or off without weight changes and reported up to +20% safety and +27% faithfulness, forcing 15 experts on and 500 off for safe steering in Qwen3-30B-A3B — and the same lever, combined with a jailbreak, drove GPT-OSS-120B from 100% to 0% safe responses ([SteerMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09660v1)). SAFEx found that disabling 12 safety experts in Qwen3-30B-A3B cut refusals by 22%, and that LoRA plus negative merging on them raised adversarial refusal from 47.7% to 81.5% with negligible AIME impact ([SAFEx](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17368)). But "one expert, one skill" is mostly wrong. Linear routers mirror hidden-state geometry, and expert overlap for the same question (about 60%) is no higher than for different questions ([arXiv 2604.09780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09780)); a pre-registered causal test on Command A+ (218B total) found only **1 of 6** hypothesized expert families cleanly selective against size-matched random nulls, although a positive control on Qwen3-30B-A3B recovered its known disjoint structure ([arXiv 2606.25092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25092)). Individual experts are polysemantic, but expert *paths* across layers are monosemantic — a colon takes different trajectories as a type annotation and as a time separator ([arXiv 2604.17837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17837)) — and RouterInterp (Oct 2026) explains routing through SAE features with about 65% better detection accuracy on gpt-oss-20b than token-statistics methods ([RouterInterp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11775)). Late layers tolerate intervention: on a model the authors name as Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, masking half the experts in layers 35–39 kept 419 of 500 outputs acceptable, while masking 30% in every layer kept only 150 of 300 ([arXiv 2608.13565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13565)). The implications are to record routing trajectories rather than individual experts, to keep super experts on a never-touch list, to treat late layers as the default intervention zone, and to require a random-expert null for every expert claim.

The evidence draws a sharp line between moving a decision and reducing variance. Single-direction steering moves discrete choices well: across twelve dense instruction-tuned models, a per-tool mean-difference vector added at 89–98% depth switched tool selection 100% of the time with five tools (random vectors of equal norm: 0%), yet steering argument values failed in all 30 attempts, uncertain queries failed 21 times more often, multi-turn τ-bench deltas ranged from −30 to +10 points, and no MoE was tested ([Tool calling](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07990v2)). Prompting still beats steering on AxBench (0.894 versus 0.165 for SAE steering in one version) ([AxBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17148)), and models partly resist steering mid-generation, recovering explicitly in 3.8% of cases on Llama-3.3-70B ([ESR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06941)). No paper measures self-consistency or answer agreement under steering. Interpretability's job in a determinism program is therefore to pin specific decision points (tool or no tool, refuse or comply, language), to protect the machinery that regulates confidence — entropy neurons ([arXiv 2406.16254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16254)), super experts and attention sinks, whose breakage causes degenerate variance — and to supply runtime telemetry such as router margins and readout gaps as abstention signals. The most direct route from features to stable behavior runs through training rather than runtime clamps: Qwen-Scope used repetition features to build negative rollouts in RL, lifting Qwen3-30B-A3B's MGSM from 76.56 to 82.40 as repetition fell ([Qwen-Scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11887v1)).

A defensible discovery program for Qwen3-30B-A3B, synthesized from these partial precedents rather than replicated from any lab, runs in six stages. Stage 0 pins batch-invariant kernels and an FP32 router, then hooks the residual stream, per-head attention, pre-top-k router logits, selected experts and weights, and per-expert outputs. Stage 1 builds a routing and expert atlas: super-expert profiling, routing frequency and paths by task and language, MoE Lens decoding and a depth-sensitivity masking map. Stage 2 reuses Qwen-Scope SAEs and trains chat-specific transcoders or a CLT only where graphs are needed, since Google DeepMind found chat-specialized SAEs closed about half the gap to dense probes ([GDM](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks)). Stage 3 maps a behavior circuit per task family — call versus no-call, tool choice, JSON validity, judgment, chain-of-thought step transitions, creative versus factual mode — from contrastive pairs, using EAP-IG over heads, experts, the shared expert and router nodes, SteerMoE-style risk-difference detection and attribution graphs confirmed by patching. Stage 4 accepts an intervention only if its off-target effect is at most a third of its on-target effect against random-expert nulls (the Command A+ protocol), it matches or beats a prompt-only baseline, it stays within a capability budget on MMLU, MGSM, IFEval and BFCL/τ-bench, and it measurably reduces variance on TARa, pass^k and paraphrase standard deviation; accepted edits are then made permanent through expert LoRA or router-bias constants. Stage 5 re-runs the atlas after every fine-tune, because super-expert locations survive post-training but behavior experts need not. On compute, Qwen3-30B-A3B's BF16 weights (about 61 GB) fit a single 80 GB GPU for hooked forward passes, but caching all 48 residual layers at width 2048 costs about 196 KB per token — roughly 20 TB per 100M tokens — which argues for on-the-fly dictionary training. No lab has published GPU-hour budgets for this kind of program on a 30B MoE.

## Prompt-to-DSL front-ends turn paraphrase variance into a parser problem

Perfect numerics deliver "same tokens, same output", not "same intent, same output". Formatting changes alone swung LLaMA-2-13B accuracy by up to **76 points**, and the sensitivity persisted with scale, more shots and instruction tuning ([FormatSpread](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324)); larger or instruction-tuned models are "not necessarily" less sensitive, while even one few-shot exemplar almost always helps ([POSIX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02185)); and on BFCL v4 the same model, Claude Opus 4.5, scored 77.47% through native function calling and 33.47% through prompting ([BFCL](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)). A DSL front-end converts this open-ended sensitivity into a closed, checkable problem, so that whole-system determinism reduces to determinism of the step from natural language to an intermediate representation (IR), followed by a pure executor.

The pattern now has both practitioner and research backing. Unmesh Joshi's July 2026 martinfowler.com essay argues that a small, constrained DSL with a deterministic validator lets an agent generate, validate and repair without a human in the loop, and that the generated program, not the prompt, becomes the source of truth ([martinfowler.com](https://martinfowler.com/articles/llm-and-dsls.html)). TypeChat models intents as a TypeScript discriminated union routed through a meta-schema, feeds validator errors back for repair and shows the user a summary generated without an LLM ([TypeChat](https://github.com/microsoft/TypeChat)). The quantitative results are striking. "Blueprint First, Model Second" encodes the procedure as code and calls the LLM only for bounded subtasks, which roughly doubled the TravelPlanner pass rate (35.56% versus 18.00%) and cut constraint violations from **275 to 11** ([arXiv 2508.02721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02721)); LLMCompiler's DAG planner ran up to 3.7 times faster and 6.7 times cheaper than ReAct ([LLMCompiler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04511v2)); Grammar Prompting found that giving the model a minimal per-example grammar helps while prepending the full grammar "did not yield any improvements" ([arXiv 2305.19234](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19234)); "Compiled AI" has the LLM write code at compile time and reached 96% task completion with zero runtime tokens, breaking even after about 17 transactions ([arXiv 2604.05150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05150)); and StructCore's deterministic compiler stage achieved "bit-for-bit reproducibility" where the LLM extraction variant drifted ([arXiv 2604.20560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20560)). Spider 2.0's 21.3% success rate on enterprise SQL is the counterweight: when the IR must reference large, messy external state, accuracy rather than determinism becomes the bottleneck, so the IR vocabulary must stay small and closed ([Spider 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07763)).

Open-source constrained decoding has consolidated on two engines, both available in vLLM and SGLang. XGrammar-2 compiles grammars more than six times faster than prior engines ([arXiv 2601.04426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04426)), and its maintainers claim up to 80 times faster compilation than v1 as tool counts grow from 10 to 500, plus "structural tags" that express tool calls, the Harmony format and reasoning channels as one protocol ([MLC blog](https://blog.mlc.ai/2026/05/04/xgrammar-2-fast-customizable-structured-generation)). llguidance reports about 50 µs of CPU per token and powers OpenAI's JSON-Schema structured outputs ([llguidance](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance)). JSONSchemaBench separates them on coverage:

| Engine | GitHub-Hard schema coverage | Kubernetes schema coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| llguidance (Guidance) | 0.41 | 0.91 | Fewest JSON Schema Test Suite failures; fast-forwarding cut time per output token below unconstrained decoding |
| llama.cpp grammars | 0.39 | — | — |
| XGrammar (v1) | 0.28 | 0.07 | Most under-constrained failure categories |
| OpenAI structured outputs | 0.09 | — | Compliance near 1.00 within its conservative supported subset |
| Outlines | 0.03 | — | Grammar compilation from seconds to ten minutes for some features |

Source: [JSONSchemaBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10868v2). XGrammar-2 postdates this benchmark, and no independent head-to-head exists.

Hosted APIs guarantee only shape, within limits. Anthropic's strict mode compiles schemas to grammars but caps each request at 20 strict tools, 24 optional parameters and 16 union-typed parameters, forbids recursion and numeric or length bounds, emits required properties first and warns that enum values "may differ from the schema only in capitalization" ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). Constrained decoding need not cost accuracy. The 2024 claim that format restrictions hurt reasoning ([Speak Freely](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442)) was largely rebutted for well-implemented constraints: JSONSchemaBench found constrained engines at or above unconstrained decoding (GSM8K 80.1% versus 83.7–83.8%), as did the .txt rebuttal ([.txt](https://blog.dottxt.ai/say-what-you-mean.html)). The real risks are grammars too narrow to leave room for reasoning — CRANE recovered up to 10 points by placing a free reasoning region before the constrained answer ([CRANE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09061v3)) — constraints misaligned with subword tokens ([DOMINO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06988)), and distortion of the output distribution. Type-constrained decoding, conversely, more than halved compilation errors in generated TypeScript ([arXiv 2504.09246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09246)).

These sources imply an IR recipe, synthesized rather than published. A closed intent enum, decoded under constraints, selects a per-intent sub-schema, following TypeChat's meta-schema and Grammar Prompting. Every slot is required but nullable, which fixes field order and avoids hosted optional-parameter limits, and enums are closed and distinct beyond case. An explicit `ambiguities` field blocks execution while non-empty, because "even the most advanced models struggle to identify and interpret ambiguity" ([AMBROSIA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19073)). Decoding is greedy on batch-invariant kernels, with an optional free reasoning prefix discarded after parsing. Output is serialized with RFC 8785 JSON canonicalization ([RFC 8785](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785)) and hashed, then passed through semantic validators with a bounded repair loop. Finally, the IR is rendered back to text by a template with no LLM, re-parsed, and required to produce the same hash — round-trip correctness is a label-free signal that correlates strongly with benchmark accuracy ([RTC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08699v2)) — and the schema is versioned, with schema, parser and engine versions in every cache key.

The parser itself can be hardened. Diverse few-shot exemplars reduce style-induced brittleness ([MOF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06969v1)), and Flip-Flop Consistency training raised agreement across prompt variants by **11.62%** without labels ([F²C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14242)). Prompt optimizers turn the prompt into a compiled artifact: DSPy's GEPA optimizer beat GRPO by 6% on average with up to 35 times fewer rollouts ([GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457)), but DSPy merely warns and proceeds when loading state saved under a different version ([DSPy docs](https://dspy.ai/tutorials/saving/)), so the lockfile has to enforce versions. IBM's PDL repository was archived on October 21, 2026 and should not become a dependency ([PDL](https://github.com/IBM/prompt-declaration-language)). Caching follows the same discipline. Exact-match memoization keyed on the canonical IR hash is safe; embedding-similarity caches are not, because correct and incorrect hits overlap in similarity. vCache adds a per-entry threshold with a user-defined error bound and reports up to 12.5 times more hits at 26 times lower error ([vCache](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v5)), yet "Similarity Is Not Validity" shows semantic caches can be poisoned ([arXiv 2609.35908](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35908v1)), so a semantic cache should only *propose* an IR that must still pass validation and the round trip. The front-end's headline metric — the rate at which a set of paraphrases maps to one IR hash — has not been published by anyone.

## Specialists with frozen interfaces beat generalists in every task family

Across all five task families, specialists of 0.6B–30B parameters match or beat much larger generalists on the narrow task, often at a tenth to a twentieth of the cost — but only inside a frozen interface. Outside it they collapse: Hammer2.1-7b scores 0% on BFCL's web-search and memory categories, and ToolACE-2-8B swings 81.5 points across prompt formats ([BFCL](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)).

| Family | Strongest documented open options (Oct 2026) | Determinism lever |
|---|---|---|
| Decision / classification | gpt-oss-safeguard-20b/120b (policy-conditioned, Apache-2.0); scalar-head classifiers such as Skywork-Reward-V2 0.6B–8B; distilled small classifiers | One forward pass to a label or scalar; a trained abstain class; calibration fine-tuning |
| Function calling | GLM-4.6 (BFCL v4 72.38%, 4th overall); xLAM-2-70b (best multi-turn score on the board); Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (τ²-bench telecom pass^4 92.1) | Native function-calling template; grammar-constrained structural tags; explicit no-call class; pass^k gates |
| Contrastive cross-evaluation | Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B (RewardBench 2 84.1); GenRM and ThinkPRM where rationales are needed; Qwen2.5-Math-PRM | Bradley-Terry scalar head; both-order pairwise judging; panels from different model families |
| Creative | Kimi K3 (EQ-Bench Elo 2082) and GLM-5.3 (2075); Qwen3.8-27B is the best small model (1671) | Diversity from enumerated candidates or seeds; deterministic selection by a pinned reward model |
| Deep reasoning | DeepSeek-V4-Pro and V4.1-Flash (MIT), Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B; dense Qwen3.8-27B; Tongyi DeepResearch-30B-A3B | Invariant or FP32 kernels; final-answer extraction and canonicalization; maj@k or DeepConf; TARa@N tracking |

Decision models should avoid sampled reasoning wherever they can. gpt-oss-safeguard (21B total, 3.6B active, fits in 16 GB) reads a developer-written policy at inference time ([gpt-oss-safeguard](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-safeguard-20b)), a flexibility that makes every decision depend on a generated chain of thought. A scalar head is cheaper and steadier: Skywork-Reward-V2 on Qwen3-0.6B already averages 70.9 across seven reward-model benchmarks ([Skywork-Reward-V2](https://huggingface.co/Skywork/Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B)), and the Kaggle LMSYS preference-prediction winner distilled 70B teachers into a Gemma2-9B student ([Nebius](https://nebius.com/blog/posts/chatbot-arena-competition-review)). Calibration and abstention must be trained, not prompted: LoRA fine-tuning on about 1,000 graded examples yields uncertainty estimates that generalize ([Kapoor et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08391)), while reasoning fine-tuning *reduced* abstention by about 24% across 20 frontier models ([AbstentionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09038v1)).

Function callers show how much the interface matters. GLM-4.6 reached 72.38% on BFCL v4 at $4.64 versus Claude Opus 4.5's 77.47% at $86.55; xLAM-2-70b has the highest multi-turn score of any model (77.38%) and asks for missing parameters instead of inventing them more often than frontier models (74.0% versus 58.0% on "Miss Param"), yet scores 15.0 on web search ([BFCL](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)). Epoch AI rated BFCL v4 "Flawed" after finding defects in 24 of 50 sampled tasks, so gaps of a few points are noise ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/berkeley-function-calling-leaderboard/review)). The reliability metric that matters is pass^k, the probability that all k trials succeed. On τ²-bench every model loses 10–30 points from pass^1 to pass^4 on retail and airline; Qwen3.5-397B-A17B holds telecom best (97.8 to 92.1), and GPT-5.2 at reasoning effort "none" falls to 22.0 airline pass^4 versus 72.0 at high effort ([τ²-bench](https://taubench.com/)).

Cross-evaluators favor small scalar reward models over prompted frontier judges. Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B posts 84.1 on RewardBench 2 and 80.0 on JudgeBench against GPT-4o's 64.9 and 59.8 ([Skywork-Reward-V2](https://huggingface.co/Skywork/Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B)). Generative verifiers win on data efficiency and out-of-domain robustness — ThinkPRM used 1% of PRM800K labels and still beat discriminative PRMs trained on all of them ([ThinkPRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16828)) — but sampling them mostly buys variance reduction rather than accuracy. Judge bias is now well characterized: style bias (0.10–0.76, favoring markdown) dominates position bias (at most 0.04) in a 2026 audit ([arXiv 2604.23178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23178)); state-of-the-art reward models fall below random (46.6%) under style-bias interference ([RM-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16184)); and different models produce strikingly homogeneous outputs, so a panel of similar judges shares the same blind spots ([Artificial Hivemind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954)). Pairwise judging in both orders is the cheapest available inconsistency detector.

Creative models separate diversity from determinism. Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3 lead open models on EQ-Bench Creative Writing v3 with low "slop" scores (9.70 and 8.42) and trail the top closed models (GPT-6-Astra 2173, Claude Opus 5 2133) by roughly 50–100 Elo ([EQ-Bench](https://eqbench.com/creative_writing.html)). Preference tuning causes mode collapse through typicality bias in human preference data, and Verbalized Sampling — prompting the model to describe a distribution over k responses — recovers 1.6–2.1 times the diversity ([Verbalized Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)); trained creative reward models beat zero-shot Claude-3.7 at judging stories (78% versus 73%) ([LitBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00769)). "Creative but reproducible" therefore means drawing diversity from an enumerated latent or a seed, selecting deterministically with a pinned reward model, and relying on seeded counter-based sampling over invariant kernels so that each seed reproduces its output exactly.

Deep-reasoning models at the open frontier are huge sparse MoEs evaluated stochastically. Kimi K3 posts GPQA-Diamond 93.5 and HLE-with-tools 56.0 ([Kimi K3](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3)), DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813 (MIT) reaches HLE-with-tools 60.0 ([DeepSeek-V4-Pro](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813)), and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (MIT) reports Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6 ([DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)) — all from vendor cards evaluated at temperature 1.0. The compact options are dense Qwen3.8-27B (GPQA 89.2) and Tongyi DeepResearch-30B-A3B, which beats o3 on HLE (32.9 versus 24.9) ([Tongyi DR](https://huggingface.co/Alibaba-NLP/Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B)). A deterministic reasoning component needs invariant kernels and answer canonicalization, and should spend its variance budget on voting: DeepConf@512 reached 99.9% on AIME 2025 with up to 84.7% fewer tokens than full parallel voting ([DeepConf](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15260)).

The best-supported architecture for the ecosystem is **one shared base with a LoRA adapter per task family** on a multi-LoRA server, plus separate full models only where a different base clearly wins, such as an 8B reward model or a frontier MoE for deep reasoning. LoRA matches full fine-tuning on SFT at Tulu3 scale, rank 1 suffices for policy-gradient RL, and it needs about a tenfold learning rate and coverage of MLP/MoE layers ([LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)); LoRA Land served 25 adapters on one A100 ([LoRA Land](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00732)); and an RL-trained 8B orchestrator beat GPT-5 on HLE at 2.5 times the efficiency ([ToolOrchestra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21689)). Two cautions apply. Batching requests for different adapters together compounds batch non-invariance, which is why vLLM replaced atomics in LoRA shrink with eight fixed K-chunks summed in order ([vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/batch_invariance/)). And production MoEs route by token, not by task: vendors ship one model with mode switches such as thinking effort, no evidence shows their experts align with these five families, and task specialization must therefore be imposed from outside — through adapters, a router over models, or Branch-Train-MiX-style construction ([BTX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07816)).

## Static route tables and durable harnesses supply the compiler behavior

The 2026 benchmarks undercut learned routers. A study of 14 models, 294 questions and 7 task types found that simply re-running the identical setup changed **5.37%** of 4,116 model–question scores, and that a static one-model-per-task-type table scored 262 of 294 at $3.33 per run versus 245 of 294 at $7.69 for the best single model, although without a held-out set ([Lee](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23023)). Three of four open-source routers evaluated on frozen tasks emitted constant or near-constant tier assignments, and a content-blind "Always-Mid" policy matched Aurelio's semantic router exactly on three benchmarks ([arXiv 2608.14641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14641)). In LLMRouterBench several recent routers failed to beat the best single model, the top cost-aware routers gained at most 4% accuracy or 31.7% cost, and OpenRouter's auto router scored **−24.7%** relative to the best single model ([LLMRouterBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07206)). Learned routers are also unstable. Injecting math or coding keywords flipped a BERT router's choice at an average **98%** rate ([arXiv 2504.07113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07113)); short, query-independent "confounder gadgets" force routing to the strong model ([arXiv 2501.01818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01818)); and OrcaRouter traced paraphrase flips to narrow top-two margins, cutting its pick-flip rate from **16.7% to 2.4%** with a tie-break rule within ε=0.02 at a cost of about 0.86 points ([OrcaRouter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30736)).

A compiler-grade router is therefore layered: a deterministic table or rule DSL maps each request to a task type, a lockfile maps each task type to a pinned specialist, optional per-instance expert recruitment handles only the hard buckets, and token-level MoE routing runs inside each model. Symbolic-MoE's recruitment gained 8.15 points and ran 16 experts on one GPU by batching queries per assigned expert ([Symbolic-MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05641)), which also makes batch composition a deterministic function of the routing plan. Ensembles should sample the best model repeatedly rather than mix weaker ones; Self-MoA beat mixed Mixture-of-Agents by 6.6% on AlpacaEval 2.0 ([Self-MoA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674)). Where a learned router is needed, Arch-Router, a 1.5B generative router, reached 93.17% routing accuracy at 51 ms — roughly matching Claude-Sonnet-3.7 — though its authors warn that overlapping policy descriptions "can lead to arbitrary routing" ([Arch-Router](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16655v1)). vLLM Semantic Router v0.3 "Themis" (June 2026) supplies the governance features a compiler needs: a routing DSL with tests and conflict detection, policy versions with shadow, activate and revert states, replay records, warnings on unknown config fields "to catch silent routing drift", and hard locks that block model switches during active tool loops ([vLLM-SR](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-06-05-v0.3-vllm-sr-themis-release)).

The harness must treat every non-self-hosted LLM call as a recorded, nondeterministic activity. OpenAI's `seed` is explicitly best-effort ([OpenAI cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/reproducible_outputs_with_the_seed_parameter)), and hosted models drift: one deployed cascade ran 179 days across unannounced version changes ([MAKE](https://doi.org/10.3390/make8100331)). Durable execution is the substrate. The Temporal and OpenAI Agents SDK integration reached general availability on March 23, 2026, running the agent loop as a deterministic workflow whose model calls are activities that "are not repeated during Workflow replay" ([Temporal](https://temporal.io/blog/announcing-openai-agents-sdk-integration)), and Pydantic AI wraps model and tool calls as Temporal activities the same way ([Temporal blog](https://temporal.io/blog/build-durable-ai-agents-pydantic-ai-and-temporal)). LangGraph's time-travel replay, by contrast, re-executes LLM calls after the checkpoint, which "may produce different results" ([LangGraph](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/javascript/langgraph/use-time-travel.md)), so it needs a cassette or cache layer to behave like a compiler. For testing, Chronicle records each nondeterministic boundary — model calls, tool calls, routing decisions — at about 23 µs, gives bit-stable full replay across 20 repetitions, and its "cut-point replay" caught every unsafe mutant where a stub-everything baseline caught none ([Chronicle](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20625)). The lockfile key should hash everything the sources identify as breaking determinism: weights, tokenizer and chat template; engine version and kernel flags (one 2026 blog reports vLLM's async scheduling can break batch invariance even with the flag set ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/vllm-batch-invariance-why-temperature-0-isnt-deterministic/))); TP/EP layout and data type; sampling parameters and seed; router policy version; and canonical prompt bytes and tool-schema hash.

Escalation follows the same rules. Confidence is computed on deterministic inference or recorded, thresholds are versioned constants with a dead band so tiny numeric drift cannot flip a tier, and escalation budgets are static to bound adversarial "forced deferral" attacks on cascades ([arXiv 2606.15308](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15308)). Calibrating to the *benefit* of escalating, not merely the cheap model's correctness, is the 2026 refinement ([Signed Rescue Routing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07786)). Verifiers that are programs — tests, schemas, type checkers — should come first, because LLM judges top out near human agreement: Shopify iterated its judges from Cohen's κ 0.02 to 0.61 against a human ceiling of 0.69 ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/building-production-ready-agentic-systems)). The clearest industry lesson is that the largest determinism gains come from *removing* LLM decisions, not from sampling more carefully. Stripe's coding agents produce over 1,300 PRs a week from code-defined "blueprints" that interleave deterministic steps (lint, push, test) with agent steps ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/stripe-autonomous-coding-agents/)), and Anthropic frames routing as a workflow pattern of "predefined code paths" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)).

## Parity needs a layered scorecard, consistency training and per-output checks

No single metric captures compiler-like parity. GPT-4o at temperature 0 produced identical raw strings across ten runs on **0%** of college-math questions (TARr@10) but identical parsed answers on **50%** (TARa@10), with accuracy ranging from 44% to 88% across runs ([Atil et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04667v5)). A usable scorecard stacks six layers: bitwise repeatability (TARr@N across batch sizes, TP sizes and GPU types, plus the first divergent token); answer repeatability (TARa@N, best-to-worst spread and pass^k); semantic equivalence (JSON canonicalization for structured output, execution or SMT checks for code, and entailment clusters for prose, as in [semantic entropy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11186750)); invariance (flip rate under a versioned library of metamorphic transformations); calibration (ECE and conformal coverage, which holds only marginally over the calibration distribution ([Mohri & Hashimoto](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10978))); and identity, via an MMD two-sample test against a frozen reference — a test that found **11 of 31** commercial Llama endpoints serving a different distribution from Meta's weights ([Model Equality Testing](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20247v2)). Inspect's `pass_k` and `majority` reducers and clustered standard errors implement much of this directly ([Inspect](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/metrics.html)). LLMs should not be the final arbiter of code equivalence: the best of 19 models reached only 63.8% and 76.2% in EquiBench's hardest categories against a 50% baseline ([EquiBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12466v3)).

Release-to-release parity is the weakest link in current practice. Base-model updates cause "negative flips" in downstream adapters, and a compatibility adapter cut them by up to 40% from Llama 1 to Llama 2 but only 1–5% across large capability jumps ([MUSCLE](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09435v2)). OpenAI's sycophancy postmortem records that offline evals "generally looked good" and A/B tests were positive before the rollback ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/)), and Anthropic's 2025 infrastructure bugs — including a routing error that hit 16% of Sonnet 4 requests at its peak — surfaced through community reports ([Simon Willison](https://feeds.simonwillison.net/2025/Sep/17/anthropic-postmortem/)). A release gate therefore needs a per-capability negative-flip budget, an MMD canary, metamorphic suites and production canaries, not just aggregate accuracy.

Training can make a model intrinsically more consistent, at a price. Invariance training uses the model's own clean-prompt answer as the target: bias-augmented consistency training cut Gemini 2.5 Flash's ClearHarm attack success from **67.8% to 2.9%** and beat SFT on stale targets by avoiding "specification staleness" and "capability staleness" ([GDM](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/DLrQ2jjijqpX78mHJ/gdm-consistency-training-helps-limit-sycophancy-and)), and Flip-Flop Consistency is the unsupervised paraphrase equivalent. Mode-seeking objectives concentrate probability mass on one answer: self-consistency preference optimization lifted Llama-3 8B by 6.5% on ZebraLogic, past Llama-3 70B ([ScPO](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/prasad25a.html)); reverse-KL on-policy distillation reached 70% on AIME'24 in about 150 steps ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)); and entropy minimization alone matched GRPO trained on 60K labels ([arXiv 2505.15134](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15134v1)). The trade-off is real. RL performance follows R = −a·e^H + b and plateaus as entropy H reaches zero ([Cui et al.](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.22617)); RLVR models lose to their base models at large k ([Yue et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13837)); and ordinary RL degrades calibration, which a Brier-score reward (RLCR) restores without losing accuracy ([RLCR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16806)). A confidently wrong low-entropy model is the worst case for a compiler, so the design implication is to keep a high-entropy "explorer" checkpoint, distill a low-entropy "compiler" checkpoint from it, and pair the latter with calibration and clarification training.

Compiler history defines the trust model. Csmith found 325+ bugs in GCC, LLVM and commercial compilers; after about six CPU-years it found no wrong-code bugs in CompCert's verified middle-end but six in its unverified front-end, and its authors concluded that "verification does not obviate testing, but rather complements it" ([Csmith](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~regehr/papers/pldi11-preprint.pdf)). The LLM equivalent is "the LLM proposes, the checker disposes": an untrusted model whose every output a small trusted checker validates. Checking LLM-transpiled Rust against a reference oracle raised the bounded-verified pass rate from 1% to 42% ([VERT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18852)); off-the-shelf models verify 82% of Dafny specs but only 44% in Verus and 27% in Lean, which makes target language a design lever ([Vericoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22908)); a runtime rule DSL prevented over 90% of unsafe code-agent executions at millisecond overhead ([AgentSpec](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18666)); and 929 catalogued bugs in llama.cpp, vLLM, DeepSpeed, MLC-LLM and TensorRT-LLM show the inference engine is this stack's unverified front-end ([arXiv 2506.09713](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09713v2)), which is why Vosti-style proofs matter. Three limits remain: ambiguity, where GPT-4's disambiguations were judged correct 32% of the time against 90% for humans ([AmbiEnt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.14399)); the entropy–capability coupling; and equivalence checking that is undecidable in general. A 2026 position paper argues deterministic inference hides uncertainty and tail risk ([Stochastic CHAOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07239)); the critique applies to greedy single-sample *evaluation*, not to determinism as a serving option. No certification standard for LLM output parity exists, nothing comparable to DO-178C tool qualification.

The reference stack that results:

| Layer | What breaks parity | Mitigation available in Oct 2026 | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kernels and hardware | Reductions that depend on batch, chunk, prefill/decode path, cache state, TP size and GPU model | Batch-invariant or verified kernels on a pinned target triple; LLM-42, MarginGate or CoRun per request | Bitwise shown on MoE at production scale; not across vendors |
| MoE router | Top-k flips at near-ties, per-batch quantization scales, combine order | FP32 router, stable tie-break, fixed capacity, FP32 slot-order combine, routing logs and replay | Strong on one stack; invariance across EP/TP sizes unproven |
| Sampling | Batch-dependent multinomial sampling | Greedy, or seeded counter-based Gumbel sampling | Shipped in SGLang |
| Front-end (prompt to DSL) | Format and paraphrase sensitivity, up to 76 points | Closed typed IR, constrained decoding, canonical hash, round trip, consistency training | IR-hash agreement under paraphrase unmeasured |
| Routing across models | Keyword (98%) and paraphrase (16.7%) flips | Static task table, margin tie-breaks, versioned policies | Benchmarks favor the static table |
| Orchestration | Re-executed calls, hosted-model drift | Durable execution, cassette and cut-point replay, lockfiles | Mature for replay; hosted APIs stay nondeterministic |
| Outputs | Right shape, wrong content | Program verifiers, property-based and metamorphic tests, formal checks where specs exist | Strong only where a spec exists |

## Conclusion

The question has shifted from "can an MoE be made deterministic?" to "deterministic with respect to what contract?" The numerics are effectively solved on a pinned target triple, at single-digit cost when kernels are co-designed, so the variance that remains is policy-level and semantic: pass^1-to-pass^4 drops of 20–30 points on agent benchmarks and 76-point swings from prompt formatting dwarf kernel effects, and nobody has yet measured how much of that agent inconsistency disappears under bitwise-deterministic serving. That measurement should be the program's first experiment, because it decides whether the engineering budget belongs in kernels or in the front-end. The architecture the evidence supports treats the LLM the way CompCert's authors treat an unverified front-end — a closed DSL and canonical hash at the boundary, static versioned routing, specialists confined to frozen interfaces, determinism offered per request rather than server-wide, and every output checked by a program wherever a spec exists.

For the MoE itself, the router is where determinism and interpretability meet. Router margins are simultaneously where flips happen and a free uncertainty signal, routing logs are the natural compilation artifact, super experts define the never-touch list, and task-masked or hash routing is the untested lever with the largest potential payoff. The highest-value open experiments follow directly: per-layer router-margin distributions, the quality cost of per-task top-k allow-lists on Qwen3-30B-A3B or Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, routing consistency under paraphrase, and whether steering or expert pinning raises TARa@N at all. Until those results exist, "deep discovery" should be judged by whether it yields telemetry and guardrails that make the compiler auditable, not by whether it produces a complete circuit map.
